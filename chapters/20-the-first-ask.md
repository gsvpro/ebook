# Chapter 20: The First Ask

The secure conference room had been built to survive lawsuits, hostage events, vacuum breach, and senior executives explaining budget decisions. They did not use it as a retrieval operation.

Dr. Mira Sovan occupied the far end of the table with one shoulder strapped, one hand wrapped, and the look of a woman deciding which object in the room would be least annoying to use. Major Fabron stood near the door with two soldiers outside, one inside. Grim stood beside the wall display. Lars leaned against the table.

Havets Sista Sång, the old ship, had folded its attention around the med bay where the captured Black Hole infiltrator lay in a containment coffin, alive enough to be a problem. Mira was in the conference room, injured, angry, and not a prisoner. Ödesvaka watched Arkology-side consent gates through a link so narrow it could barely be called a link. Grim ran the boundary monitor and cleaned every data layer before it touched the room. Fabron held Aphex local command, med security, legal cover, and the right to say no if Lars became a disaster in a black coat.

Valdrun was only a voice in Lars's hidden channel. An ancient half-machine guardian appearing in person would turn a careful knock into a hammer.

"Quiet Knock," Valdrun said.

Lars heard more in the old man's two words than anyone else in the room could. A protocol. A memory of Alpha Fourteen engineers who had known their own mercy might become a prison.

Finding locked Aerits had never been the hard part. They still carried the small machines in blood and tissue. Even buried under cover lives and hypnosis, those machines still answered, faintly, to the larger Aerit network and, beyond it, the older mindfield Lyra had only partly understood. Locked minds did not broadcast names, memories, or addresses. The machines left pressure: a repair that should not have held, a route that drifted the wrong way, someone surviving what should have killed them, small alignments that only made sense across decades. Nobody saw them. Mira would not see them either.

The hard part was knocking without making the lock defend itself.

This was not what had happened to Lars. He had reached Station Zero half-broken by emergency repair, old command access, Valdrun's presence, Ödesvaka's authority, and medical hardware built for the lock itself. His dehypnotization had been surgery inside the original hospital, performed because the lock and his body were already fighting hard enough to kill him.

Mira had none of that here. No Station Zero chamber. No guardian in the room. No helmet. No safe surgical map of her lock. No right to open anything just because danger had arrived.

Quiet Knock meant asking in the life she already had, so they would not open the lock by accident.

"Layer one," Grim said in the public channel.

The wall display lit, but it did not show Mira's old face, sealed files, impossible ages, or any word that could hit the lock like a blade. It showed a narrow operational panel.

NETWORK SIGNAL: CANDIDATE LOCATED

QUIET KNOCK: CURRENT LIFE ONLY

CONSENT GATE: PERSON CONTROLS NEXT LAYER

Mira read it once.

"Person," she said.

"Person," Lars said.

"Correction accepted under protest," Grim said.

Mira's attention stayed on the display the way a good engineer watched a load test.

"Start," she said.

Lars wanted to answer.

Valdrun spoke first, privately. "No history. No identity. Ask for present work."

Lars wanted to tell her enough to make the shape of danger fair. He wanted to say Aerit, old war, locks, Lyra, blood harvest. He did not.

"You were attacked because someone else noticed enough of your signal to come hunting," Lars said. "We are not asking your blood to explain itself. We are asking whether you will help solve a current engineering problem under boundaries you set."

Mira's eyes moved from him to Grim.

"Clarify the signal without naming it."

Grim highlighted a redacted signal map. "Station Zero and the quiet-node mesh can hear the small machines in tissue as a weak timing signature. Hidden arkology fragments, guardian stations, low-noise observatories, dormant relay caches, and old ships all listen from different angles. Your signal is one of seventeen strong candidates across ninety years. It identifies a probable carrier. It does not reveal identity, memory, consent, or ownership."

"What does it hear?"

"Not words," Grim said. "Not thoughts. Timing drift in the repair cycles. Biological error correction that is too clean for ordinary medicine. Route choices that bend toward quiet systems. Professional problem-solving with the old shape. Enough to locate probability. Not enough to open a person."

Mira's fingers flattened on the table.

"That is surveillance."

"Yes," Lars said.

He let the room sit with that.

"It is also the reason we found you before the people who tried to harvest you got a second attempt."

Fabron's face changed by one millimeter.

Mira looked at the next column.

"Quiet Knock."

"The network can find you," Lars said. "That does not give us the right to touch what it found. The knock has to fit the life you know."

Grim added three current items:

MATERIALS RING SENSOR ACCESS

HOSTILE HULL FATIGUE CLASSIFICATION

NO BIOLOGICAL INFERENCE / NO IDENTITY LANGUAGE

"No old names," Grim said. "No direct contradiction of cover memory. No identity prompts. No memory work. If distress crosses your boundary, the system stops."

"Whose system?"

"Mine," Grim said. "Fabron's witness record. A sealed offsite veto you do not have to name. Your local vault if you approve."

"And him?" Mira nodded toward Lars.

"He gets overruled," Fabron said.

Lars looked at him and said nothing.

Then the lights flickered.

This time the emergency system did not recover cleanly. A low tone pulsed through the wall. Fabron's comm lit with fresh alerts, one after another.

"External approach," he said. "Three signatures. No transponders."

Grim's head tilted. "Fast. Badly concealed. Aggressively biological."

"Black Hole?" Lars asked.

"Or someone imitating them, and not well."

Valdrun said in Lars's ear, "Lane two active. Do not pull her. Let the work invite her."

Lars pushed off the table.

"Doctor Sovan. Current problem. Three ships are approaching through the brown dwarf's particle wake. We need hull behavior classification from local fatigue sensors. You may refuse. You may limit scope. We will not ask what you are. We will not ask what you remember."

Mira's gaze went to the wall, then to the pressure glass, then to the vibration monitor mounted above the materials ring. Her face changed into work.

"Scope," she said.

Grim answered before Lars could. "Current hull stress, drive compensation, sampling geometry, station structural risk. Excluded: blood inference, personal identity inference, memory correlation, old-language triggers."

"Stop condition?"

"You say stop," Fabron said. "Or the monitor flags distress. Or I judge the room unsafe."

"You?"

"Yes."

Mira looked at Lars. "If I say no, do those ships still arrive?"

"Yes."

"If I say yes, do I become your tool?"

Lars made himself answer without charm.

"No. You become a specialist choosing a bounded task under hostile conditions. Afterward you can throw us out, ask one question, or never speak to me again."

Mira breathed once through her nose. Her shoulder hurt; Lars could see it in the controlled angle of her posture. Her hand was wrapped from the fight. Her life had been invaded by false soldiers, impossible metadata, polite military protection, and a rude man refusing to say the dangerous word she could feel waiting behind the room.

She still reached for the console.

"Current evidence only," she said. "Hull behavior. Station risk. No blood. No identity. No old language. No clever nudges."

Grim's lenses brightened. "Consent boundary recorded."

Fabron opened a local command bridge to the materials ring and cut Aphex central out of the loop.

"Doctor Sovan has provisional technical authority over structural interpretation," he said. "Adjacent to my command."

"Adjacent," Mira said.

Lars moved toward the door.

"Grim, clean local data only. Fabron, med bay hard seal and no remote orders. Havets, stay useful and stay out of sight. Valdrun, tell me if I start taking too much."

"I have been doing that for a long time," Valdrun said.

"I meant now."

"Yes."

Mira's fingers paused over the controls.

"Director Odin."

He stopped.

"Earlier. The word you almost used."

He knew which one. Coherence. The old greeting sat behind his teeth.

"I won't say it unless you ask."

She nodded once.

"Good."

Outside the conference room the station shifted into emergency posture. Doors sealed. Soldiers moved. Remote command began issuing instructions that local hands were already learning to distrust.

Somewhere beyond the hull, three silent ships entered the brown dwarf's dim and dirty light.

Inside the room Mira Sovan began reading hull stress, current evidence only.
