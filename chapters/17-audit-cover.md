# Chapter 17: Audit Cover

The brown dwarf hung ahead of Fastulv as a swollen ember in infrared. Around it the old mining claims, research platforms, and corporate stations had learned to look boring, mostly for tax reasons. Aphex Defence called the facility Dependency L-Prime 41.

Fastulv was the ship they were meant to see: Lars's new freight hull, lights on, transponder shouting. Havets Sista Sång waited out of ordinary space. Lars could not see the old ship on any display. He knew it was there because a cold command-access sat in the back of his mind, like a hand on a rail. Grim sat in the copilot's chair and ran Fastulv's navigation. He did not run Havets. The old ship had its own mind.

On the forward display the Aphex facility grew: three habitation cylinders, two research spines, a shielded materials ring, a small defense dock, and a corporate chapel module.

Valdrun's voice came through the hidden channel from Havets.

"Remember the plan."

"I remember. Audit cover. Consent before unlock. Local intervention only. We do not start a war."

Aphex traffic control hailed them. A nervous woman, with the brittle calm of someone reading from emergency procedure while senior management watched.

"Fastulv, this is Dependency L-Prime 41 Control. Confirm identity and audit authorization."

Lars opened the board-level packet Gabriel Arsenault had sent three hours earlier. It contained enough signatures, seals, emergency clauses, legal threats, and French phrasing to keep a lawyer busy for a week.

"L-Prime Control, this is Captain Lars Odin, Aphex Defence board director, strategic integrity audit authority under post-attack emergency powers. Arriving with one freighter and one robot."

The channel was silent for half a second too long.

"Confirmed, Director Odin. Please hold for security escort."

"All right. Holding."

A military cutter detached from the defense dock and came toward them. The transponder said Aphex Defence, Eleventh Brigade.

Major Elliot Fabron appeared on the comm a moment later, helmet off, uniform immaculate, already looking tired.

"Monsieur Odin."

"Major Fabron. I read your report."

"I stand by it."

"I wrote the cover language. You are my assigned security liaison for the duration of this audit."

"Did you volunteer?"

"No."

"All right."

Fabron's cutter escorted them toward Dock Two. Lars let him. The facility's scanners washed over Fastulv: hull, cargo, declared weapons, undeclared weapons that looked like maintenance systems. At least six of those should have set off alarms. They did not, because Grim had already gone through the scan tables and told Fastulv's systems what to answer.

Inside the hidden channel Valdrun said, "Fabron is watching how you handle the scans."

"He always watches."

"He is better than last time."

"Last time he was trying to rescue me from people I had already killed."

The docking clamps engaged.

Lars stood, adjusted his black coat, checked that the Ragnarrök was powered down, and looked at Grim.

"Simple service automaton."

Grim's servos whined. "Audit support."

"And food."

"And food."

The airlock opened.

Fabron waited on the other side with eight armored Aphex soldiers, two legal observers, one medical compliance officer, and a station administrator who looked as if she had slept in a chair designed by accountants.

Fabron glanced at the Ragnarrök.

"You brought that."

"Yes."

"This is a research facility."

"I know."

The administrator stepped forward. "Director Odin, I am Administrator Celia Marchand. We have prepared the audit suite, personnel records, laboratory access, and secure conference rooms."

"Good. I want three things first. Full list of personnel with unusual medical exceptions over the last ninety years. The materials fatigue labs. And whatever passes for steak on this facility."

Marchand blinked. "Steak?"

Fabron closed his eyes a moment.

They led him through the facility's main spine. L-Prime 41 smelled of filtered metal, coffee concentrate, anxious humans, and cold research money. Aphex branding was everywhere, but less aggressively than on the public stations.

Lars saw the stress points at once. One power loop ran everything through a single switch. Two emergency corridors had been rerouted so executives could lock them. The medical archive took too long to answer, longer than the privacy policy claimed. The materials ring was shielded well enough against radiation from outside, and poorly enough against someone already inside.

Workers had added manual overrides where the corporate design preferred locks. They had put food gardens in ventilation dead spaces, extra tool caches that were not on any official list, unofficial mutual-aid boards, and handwritten maintenance tags with tiny spiral marks in the corners. Återviens habits, not named and not official, but alive.

Lars did not look at Fabron.

Fabron noticed anyway.

The audit suite overlooked the materials ring through pressure glass. A dozen researchers moved below among test rigs, vacuum chambers, stress tables, and fatigue simulators. One of them stood beside a cracked composite beam, arguing with three engineers and a wall display. On the record she was forty-two.

She had dark copper skin, close-cropped silver hair, and the stillness of someone who did not waste movement. Her ID tag read DR. MIRA SOVAN, MATERIALS FATIGUE SPECIALIST.

She tapped the cracked beam once and said something that made all three engineers stop arguing.

Lars could not hear through the glass. He knew the shape of the gesture anyway.

Grim's private voice entered his head. Candidate probability just rose.

Lars watched Dr. Mira Sovan turn away from the beam. For a brief moment she looked up through the pressure glass, directly at him. No recognition. But her hand went to the back of her neck.

Fabron said quietly, "Is that who you came for?"

Lars did not turn.

"Major."

"Should I be protecting her from you?"

Lars finally looked at him. Fabron's face was stiff and formal. His eyes were not.

Lars said, "Possibly."
