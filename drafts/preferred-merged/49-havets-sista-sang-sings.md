# Chapter 49: Havets Sista Sång Sings

Havets Sista Sång did not sing in sound.

The old ship changed when things happened. A pump alarm here. A false all-clear there. A door that would not take a remote order. Gravity that sat a little wrong, so a captain looked at his own instruments instead of the fleet board. Nothing visible fired.

At First Door, the old Creator station floated in darkness. The ring did not rotate, but every sensor insisted it wanted to. Its outer ribs carried 600,000-year-old repair scars. Its inner aperture showed nothing.

Black Hole pods came by the hundreds, carrying stolen tissue, biological processors, and enough desperate computation to force a bad question. They did not fly like a fleet. They drifted as clinics, pilgrims, fuel drones, lab pods, funeral craft, and damaged refugees. Each body was too small to justify a warship's full attention. Together they made a net.

Farther out, Syren core ships held formation for synchronized silence. Millions had become thousands because the public lie and Vigor's practical trouble had broken the easy wave. Thousands were still too many if they breathed as one. Aphex cutters and NUKEA power vessels lurked at jurisdictional edges, every captain waiting for someone else to make responsibility easier.

Fastulv arrived visible, loud, insulting.

That was the point. Fastulv burned a course any half-competent predator could read: newly built freight hull, board-level Aphex access, Lars Odin's blood, Grim's presence, Fabron's official authority. Lars had built the ship himself, months of lab and asteroid mining and illegal bazaar parts, and he let the public feeds see him in the cockpit of that visible bait. He let Aphex see his transponder. He let Black Hole intercept enough chatter to think the ancient Aerit hull was still far behind.

Havets Sista Sång was already inside the field geometry.

It sat where old systems still let it in: in the dark behind other ships' mass, in maintenance doors no Creator system remembered giving away. Fastulv was bait. Grim sat in Fastulv and ran the decoy drones. He did not run Havets. The old ship had its own mind, older and sharper than a year-old android, and it did not take orders from him.

Lars opened a public channel. "This is Captain Lars Odin of Fastulv. You are blocking an emergency lane. Back off."

Fabron, in the copilot chair, said the wording was not approved.

"I know."

Grim occupied Fastulv and three decoy drones. He was stretched thin and he liked the work. Mira's model ran through a secure remote array. Her consent boundary included high-stress analysis and excluded identity prompts. Ren Koss piloted the service route from a protected cradle, partial memories guiding small ships through field eddies no modern nav believed in. His husband sat just outside the medical frame, pretending not to count Ren's breaths.

"Route drift," Ren said.

"Define drift," Fabron asked.

"The door is pulling toward the shortest path. Take the ugly curve instead."

Fabron did not like it. He flew it.

Vigor's feeds arrived as mundane fragments. Soup stations. Pump checks. Names read out of order. A mother tying a child's boot. A reactor crew counting bolts in three languages. Mother Aveline hitting a microphone because it fed back during her hymn about batteries.

Grim said the song was terrible. Vigor said it was working.

Havets waited until everyone brightened.

Brightness here was not light. It was how many people were watching the same thing at the same time. Black Hole began forced coupling. Pods linked through blood processors, forming one hunger across many bodies. Syren began synchronized silence, thousands pressing their breath into one shape. Aphex sensors focused. NUKEA stabilizers charged. First Door woke wrong.

Mira's model turned from warning amber to a color she had refused to name.

"Too many people are watching the Door at once," she said. "The field is getting stiff. The Creator layer is trying to become a central hub."

"Older layer?" Lars asked.

"Pulling back."

Then Havets sang.

Every Black Hole pod received a local maintenance emergency. Not false. Havets found real faults: overheating pumps, contaminated growth gel, unstable processor tissue, cracked coolant loops, bad seals, bad batteries, borrowed organs, lies in the thermal budget. It amplified each local problem until the pod's own survival routines demanded attention. The great hunger fractured into smaller panics.

A pod disguised as a clinic had to choose between maintaining the forced coupling rhythm and stopping its oxygen scrubber from poisoning three real patients. A lab drone dropped out to keep processor gel from cooking. A funeral craft cut its link because its stolen tissue began rejecting the command pulse.

Every Syren relay received unsynchronized human noise. Not jamming. Local acts. A child reciting names of dead miners. A reactor crew counting bolts. Vigor chopping onions. Mother Aveline leading a terrible hymn about batteries. Pail telling people to breathe at their own pace.

Every Aphex and NUKEA ship received liability, local hazard data, and enough accurate contradictory safety flags that captains had to make decisions instead of obeying fleet dashboards. One Aphex cutter broke formation to pull a damaged pilgrim shuttle out of a shear pocket. A NUKEA vessel dumped stabilizer charge into local shielding instead of the fleet bus. A captain who would have loved a heroic order had to answer a mechanic standing beside the manual governor.

Havets did not command them.

First Door flickered. The Creator hardware tried to become a hub. The older anchor underneath withdrew from force and opened to distributed witness. For a moment, the dark around the station filled with no light at all and every passive sensor recorded structure. Seven archives would later assemble it.

Black Hole adapted again.

Its hidden predator ship had never joined the pod swarm. It had waited cold behind an ore shadow, running on stolen Creator plating, biological processors, Aphex tactical code, NUKEA power logic, and Syren entrainment patterns harvested from Door Nine.

The predator fired a biological lance toward First Door.

The lance carried concentrated stolen nanobot tissue and a set of orders meant to run those machines. It did not aim to destroy the station. It aimed to make the older Door answer under pain.

"Intercept," Fabron said.

Fastulv could not. Too slow. It was in the visible lane because visibility had bought the hidden lane time. Lars could burn the engines red, fire every weapon, and still arrive after the lance touched the aperture.

Havets could, but only by revealing itself in a way every sensor might see.

The old ship's hidden capacity had a cost. If it reached into the lance the way it had reached into the probe at L-Prime, the sensors would see extra mass where there should be none, a tug on nearby ships, a contradiction in the quiet arrays, and enough of a silhouette that Aphex, NUKEA, Syren, and Black Hole survivors would know something old and not owned had moved. Once seen, Havets could never again be only rumor.

Lars felt the old ship ask without words.

He opened the distributed channel. "Havets can stop the lance. Exposure risk high. Local votes, now."

Mira: yes. Record exposure as cost, not precedent.

Fabron: yes. Civilian harm outweighs secrecy. I dislike this sentence.

Valdrun: yes. We have hidden long enough to remember why hiding mattered.

Ödesvaka, from Arkology Zero: authorization granted, exposure objection logged, future containment review mandatory.

Vigor: sing, old ship; we'll mend the fence after.

Ren: clear lane. Ugly curve holds for two seconds.

Grim: yes. 1.8 seconds is too long.

Lars: yes, damn it.

Havets Sista Sång became visible for 1.8 seconds.

Aphex sensors filed it under weather before surrendering. The old ship crossed the lance's path. It did not fire a gun. There was nothing to see, no beam, no flash. It reached into the lance the way it had reached into the probe at L-Prime. The programs that were supposed to keep the stolen tissue pointed at the Door began to give opposite orders. The lance lost its shape and turned to dust.

First Door dimmed.

The cost landed immediately. Aphex central screamed for classification. NUKEA sensors threw power-safety panics across three fleets. Syren feeds clipped the impossible dark and called it proof of a hidden angel until Pail's network drowned them in unsynchronized repair noise. Black Hole's predator ship marked Havets as real and turned every surviving sensor toward the old sea.

Millions saw only sensor failure, a flash of impossible dark, and Lars Odin laughing.

The laugh was deliberate. If the feeds wanted a visible cause, he would give them a loud idiot in Fastulv and keep their eyes away from the old ship.

The main Black Hole ship turned toward Fastulv.

Fabron looked at the targeting board. "Your bait is working."

"Visible bait."

"I will write that in the report."

Lars grinned because fear needed somewhere to go.
