# Chapter 25: Vigor's Table

Vigor did not own a throne, a headquarters, or a fleet.

He owned kitchens.

His main hall on The Rim had once been a cargo sorting deck. The old rails still ran in the floor, half-hidden under rugs and patched plating. Now the deck held long tables, hydroponic herb towers, fermentation tanks, illegal cheeses aging under false customs labels, off-duty Aphex soldiers, NUKEA technicians hiding from their supervisor, sober smugglers, station maintenance guild elders, and a wall of farm tools.

The air was hot with yeast, pepper, wet wool, machine oil, and bodies. Somewhere in the back a goat complained.

Lars arrived hungry and suspicious.

"Good," Vigor said, embracing him hard enough to threaten ribs. "Sit. You can glare after soup."

"That sounds medically illegal."

"Only for cowards and committee doctors. Sit."

Vigor was broader than most doorways. He wore a flour-streaked apron over a patched pressure vest. His beard had collected something green. He smelled like smoke, dill, and work.

Fabron stood near the entrance. "I am on duty."

Vigor looked him over. "Then you need soup before civilians. You look hollow."

"I do not require feeding to perform command functions."

"That is what men say right before they fall over."

The soup arrived before Fabron could refuse. Grim received a bowl and examined it, then ate, then went very still.

"This contains smoke, salt, and fungal ester."

"Then it is working," Vigor said.

The room looked chaotic. Lars knew better. Vigor had arranged tables by trust, supply route, old grudges, and deniability. Aphex soldiers sat beside smugglers they had once arrested and now had to ask for fuel. Farmers sat beside reactor technicians. A maintenance elder from Abantu Station Twelve sat where she could see both exits and the dessert hatch. A Syren sympathizer named Pail sat where three grandmothers could watch him.

Food moved. So did information.

No one stood up to brief the room. Bowls crossed tables with scraps of news tucked under bread. A jar of preserved lemons moved from a Havskant mechanic to a Rim fuel broker and came back with a shipping discrepancy etched on its lid. Two Aphex corporals argued loudly about cards while quietly confirming that Fabron's local authority order had spread farther than Aphex central liked.

Vigor ladled soup.

"Eat," he told a young NUKEA technician whose hands would not stop shaking. "Then tell Rauk's people which supervisor signs from fear and which one signs from greed."

"I should not be here," the technician whispered.

"Most useful people shouldn't. Eat anyway."

The L-Prime coordinate family matched three supply disappearances, two NUKEA recall anomalies, and one abandoned Creator-era survey buoy bought at auction by a charity front connected to Syren. The facts arrived in pieces: a smuggler's route, a chapel pantry ledger, a reactor guild warning, a school archive that had recorded an old buoy because children liked strange shapes.

"Syren changed tone," Vigor said when enough pieces lay on the table. "Less office sermon, more hunger talk. They say the KBC Void wants surrender."

Lars groaned. "The universe has one quiet room and some idiot starts selling tickets."

Pail stood.

He was thin, young enough to still be angry at the wrong parts of pain, and dressed in a clean white jacket that had been mended at the cuffs. His hands shook. The grandmothers became very interested in their spoons.

"You mock what you fear," Pail said. "The old silence invites surrender."

Conversations lost steam. A child was quietly moved behind a herb tower.

"Listening is fine," Lars said. "Repair is what you do when listening stops being an excuse to sit."

"Then feed us truth, not scraps."

"No."

Half the people there had risked homes, records, and names on fragments. They wanted the whole shape.

Pail's face tightened. "You keep knowledge like kings."

"Kings love crowds," Lars said. "I am trying to prevent one."

"Convenient."

"Yes."

Vigor leaned forward, both hands around a ladle big enough to settle a bar fight.

"Boy, I run kitchens on stations with bad pumps. Strange noises get a wrench, not a prayer. Check the bolts before children sleep under the roof."

"You do not understand hunger."

The old farmers near Vigor changed the way they held their spoons.

"I have buried hungry people," Vigor said. "I have stretched goat milk with water and lied to children by smiling. Do not bring your ache into my kitchen and call it authority."

Pail looked away first.

He fled before dessert.

Fabron wanted him detained. He said so in the pantry corridor, voice low and formal, while three children ran past carrying empty bowls.

"He is an exposed vector for Syren influence."

Vigor stacked bowls with more force than necessary. "He came hungry in a bad way. Do not cage him for repeating a line. Watch who fed it to him, and who asks for his report."

"He may return with worse men."

"Then the grandmothers will watch the door, and you will stand where your stiff neck is useful."

"This is not a security plan."

"It is a kitchen."

After midnight, when the hall had thinned to sleepers, washers, and quiet deals, Vigor took Lars into cold storage. The door sealed behind them. Frost edged the ceiling. Slabs of lab-grown beef hung beside real goat, labeled honestly.

Behind the meat racks a hidden screen woke.

It showed kitchens, farms, chapel pantries, mess halls, fuel depots, cold rooms, station guild workshops, and seed banks across twenty systems. Some channels were encrypted. Some were delivery notes with deliberate misspellings. Some were old women gossiping with suspicious regularity about spare filters and which local official had started talking like central command had bought his spine.

"Återviens without the word," Vigor said. "Some know a little. Most know only this: when central orders go stupid, local hands keep babies warm and goats milked. Do not put your name on my pantry, Lars."

"I was not planning to label your pantry."

"Men who become symbols lose control of the schedule. Then everyone waits for them while work piles up."

The cold made Lars's knuckles ache. Archive records sat under the skin: old hunger after Pluto, Heliostat ration fights, Lyra on a recording laughing because someone had preserved yeast through a bombardment.

"I'm trying not to become a command center," Lars said.

"Trying is not enough. Keep people close enough to slap your hand away when you start enjoying command."

"You volunteering?"

"I have a ladle with your name on it."

A message arrived from Mira, clipped and clean. Grim routed it to the cold-room screen.

The abandoned Creator buoy had awakened when exposed to the L-Prime burst.

It was not transmitting to Black Hole.

It was asking for a map.

Vigor wiped his hands on his apron and looked at the hanging meat, the hidden channels, the little lights of kitchens.

"Maps help," he said. "Then someone still has to check the stove."
