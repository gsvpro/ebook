# Chapter 21: Containment Drill

Three ships came in out of the dim heat of the brown dwarf. They were running without engine flares, and they came in through the dust, so the station saw them late. Dependency L-Prime 41 was a mining post: materials labs, crew berths, refineries, repair yards. The filters smelled of hot metal and old coolant.

Mira was in the materials lab, which they were using as a sensor room. She had the hull-stress screen up, the same kind of plot she used on test beams, and the numbers on it were bad. Something out there was pressing on the station's outer skin in a way a rock or a lost barge did not.

"Black Hole," Lars said.

Fabron came on the tactical channel. "Director, I need identification I can put in the log."

Mira did not look away from the console. She was still locked. She had no old Aerit channels, no feed from Arkology Zero, only the current facts she had agreed to look at. Two soldiers had wedged a table against the door. A tea bulb floated beside a rack of test coupons. The emergency lights went red every four seconds.

Grim's monitor stayed green. It was watching the hull only: no blood, no identity, no memory.

"Still inside what I agreed to," Mira said.

"Logged," Grim said.

Fabron spoke to the station. "All local detachments, containment posture. Confirm orders by hand. If headquarters sends you something that contradicts what you can see, you ask me first."

"Major," an Aphex lieutenant said, "that is not regulation."

"Ask me first anyway."

Fastulv left Dock Two with its lights on, its transponder shouting, and its weapons where anyone could see them. Fastulv was Lars's new freight ship. He wanted the attackers looking at it, not at the station.

Havets Sista Sång stayed hidden. It was the old Aerit ship, and it did not show on Aphex sensors. It worked through the ordinary guts of the station, the parts headquarters liked to run from far away. On one deck a cook saw a freezer light blink twice and closed the manual fire doors before the remote panel woke. In a crawlspace a pipefitter felt the vibration in a pipe change and turned a brass wheel by hand.

Doors stopped taking orders from headquarters. Shutters had to be wound shut by hand. The emergency lights changed pattern, and the workers already knew what the pattern meant.

"The station is ignoring remote commands," Grim said.

"Good," Fabron said.

The first hostile ship launched a small drone at the materials ring, the big circular lab structure that hung off the station. The drone stayed cold until it was close, then a mouth opened in its nose.

"The drone has opened a sampler," Mira said. "It is going for the warmest part of the ring, where the hull is thinnest. If it gets through, it takes air first, then it can take blood. Our point-defense guns are loaded, but they are still waiting for a go-ahead from headquarters."

"Headquarters has not cleared the shot," Grim said.

"Fastulv," Lars said.

The freight ship rolled, dumped two empty cargo pods, and heated them so they looked like a panicked evacuation. The drone turned toward the warmer target. Fastulv's belly gun cut the drone apart. The flash went white across the lab windows, then the shutters came down.

People cheered in three rooms.

"Focus," Fabron said.

The second hostile ship stayed farther out and sent a longer probe toward the air intakes of the materials ring. If that probe reached the ventilation, it could sample everyone on the lab deck from inside the pipes.

Mira leaned in.

"I can make the ring look damaged," she said. "Wrong heat, a fake crack in the braces. A probe that is hunting damage would come closer to look. If I get it wrong, we lose a brace and three labs. If I get it right, the sampler opens where we can see it."

"Your call," Lars said.

She looked at him, then nodded.

"Grim. Give me the raw sensor feeds. Take the extra labels off so I can see the heat."

"Done."

She sat at the console and worked the heat on one brace of the materials ring until the sensors said it was failing, then she marked a crack into the stress plot the same way she would have marked a real fatigue line on a test beam. To make the numbers match the metal she had to load that brace for real, not only on the screen, and the ring creaked through the lab floor for three minutes. Somewhere on an open radio a child started crying, and someone shut that channel. Lars stood in Fastulv's docking airlock with his helmet on and waited.

The probe came in after that, toward the brace she had stressed. The mouth in its nose opened wider.

Mira watched the screen. "The sampler is open. If something is going to stop it, now."

Havets Sista Sång, still hidden, did not fire a gun. There was nothing to see, no beam, no flash. The old ship took over the probe's steering, the way a thief takes over a locked car: from inside the controls, not by hitting it. The probe jerked left, then right, as if two people were fighting over the wheel. The sampler mouth slammed shut, opened again, and the whole probe whipped around so hard it dragged the ship that had sent it. That ship's side swung around in front of Fastulv.

Lars fired from the airlock. The shot hit. The enemy ship broke apart and tumbled away into the dust, leaking air and coolant.

The third hostile ship turned and ran.

Fabron ordered pursuit. Lars told Fastulv to hold.

"That ship will report how we answered," Fabron said.

"Let it," Lars said. "I want them to report Fastulv, and that we did not open Dr. Sovan."

On the lab screen the third ship shrank into the dust.

Mira looked up. There was a smear of blood under one nostril from the bad air. "Did that help?"

"Only with what you agreed to," Lars said. "Nothing else."

Soldiers counted injuries and opened stuck doors. An apprentice had a broken wrist from sealing a valve by hand. A med tech vomited in a corridor. Lars passed two officers arguing with headquarters. Fabron was on their speaker, telling them they could file a complaint after they explained why a remote queue had tried to stop them shooting a sampler they could see with their own eyes.

Then the med bay alarm went.

The infiltrator from the last attack was in a sealed coffin under three kinds of quarantine. It died without anyone touching it. The body folded in on itself. Flesh and black grit collapsed to the floor of the box. For half a second the glass showed another face, not the one that had been in there.

The medical archive screens lit with traffic nobody had authorized.

"Stop it," Lars said.

"Trying," Grim said. "It used the death to send a signal."

Havets closed three archive routes. Med guards cut two more cables by hand. Mira, still in the lab, saw the archive server housing heat too fast and told someone to kick the cooling unit until the casing cracked, so the machine would shut down.

"That is not tactical doctrine," Fabron said.

"Kick the cooling unit," Mira said.

A corporal did it.

Part of the signal still got through.

Not all of it. Enough to read. Black grit on the coffin floor. The smell of burnt antiseptic. Grim decoded the first layer: a set of coordinates pointing toward the KBC Void.

Lars looked at the grit.

"Containment drill," Fabron said.

"Write it that way," Lars said.
