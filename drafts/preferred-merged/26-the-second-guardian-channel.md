# Chapter 26: The Second Guardian Channel

The signal came through a kitchen freezer because Vigor distrusted elegant communications.

One moment Lars stared at frozen goat. The next, frost on the back wall rearranged into seven broken circles and a line of old Aerit compressed writing that made his restored memory lean forward.

No pain spike. No locked-door recoil. Only weight under the ribs.

Condensation trembled on meat hooks. Somewhere beyond the insulated walls, the last dishes from Vigor's midnight meal clattered in warm water.

Lars did not touch the frost.

"Grim," he said.

"Already isolating the freezer," Grim said through the wall speaker.

Vigor folded his arms. "Freezers keep secrets better than conference rooms."

"Freezers also keep goat."

"Exactly."

Valdrun joined from Havets Sista Sång. His image appeared as a narrow projection on the freezer door. Frost-glare cut his old face. The old ship stayed hidden; Valdrun was speaking from it. From Arkology Zero, Ödesvaka layered protocol verification around the packet. Each check stamped in the air as a formal line of old authorization. Grim ran the noise through his quantum routines.

"Authentic," Grim said. "Also compromised, or close to it."

"Those are not the same category," Fabron said. He had arrived still buttoning his coat, sidearm visible.

"They overlap," Grim said.

Vigor grunted. "Bad pantry."

The sender identified as Guardian Channel Two.

It was a relay identity built before the collapse, associated with one of the unnamed guardian functions scattered after Alpha Fourteen. It carried old routing marks, but no full name. It knew things a modern system should not know. It also arrived through civilian networks.

The content was brief.

Hold small hinges.
Creator transport layer is waking.
KBC alignment not shelter alone.
Do not gather the sleepers centrally.
Coherence?

Valdrun answered first. "Coherence uncertain."

Ödesvaka added from Arkology Zero, "Record state: partial. Compliance with central gathering prohibition: currently maintained. Suspicion level: deserved."

"It asked for a status phrase," Lars said.

"Protocol survives enthusiasm," Ödesvaka replied from Arkology Zero.

The channel offered coordinates for a dead survey platform near the awakened Creator buoy. The checksum used guardian knowledge no modern system should hold, but the route passed through civilian networks, farm cooperatives, and one children's educational archive on Havskant. The archive had stored the packet in a lesson about extinct orbital shapes because a teacher two generations ago had liked strange machines and refused to delete old data.

"Distributed," Vigor said. "Like a good root cellar."

"Or compromised through distributed systems," Fabron said. "A supply chain can be poisoned."

Vigor pointed a thick finger at him. "That is why you smell the jars."

"My military education lacked jar-smelling."

"Explains plenty."

Mira requested raw data from L-Prime. She looked exhausted, shoulder brace visible. The lab behind her had new manual locks and an Aphex guard outside who had learned to knock.

"Before anyone says anything old at me," she said, "do not."

"Wasn't going to," Lars said.

Grim sent her the packet with warnings about how hard it might load her head, and the identity markers stripped into layers so the screens would not try to name her. She took nine minutes. No one interrupted. The freezer hummed. Fabron received two calls from Aphex central and declined both.

"It rode harmless maintenance telemetry," Mira said at last. "Ordinary repair data, the kind a freezer or a pump sends every hour. There is a second carrier underneath. The path used the quiet gaps, the places nobody was watching, and it used them before the network even got stressed enough to make those gaps."

The quiet region again. Low survey. Low noise. Low attention.

"Not shelter alone," Lars said.

"That phrase is doing work," Mira said. "Too much work for one line. Either the quiet regions are useful to the Creator transport layer, or something older used those regions and the Creators copied the geography."

"Evidence?"

"Carrier timing. Timing, not wonder."

They argued for three hours in the cold and then in the kitchen because Vigor threatened to beat them with a ladle if they made the goat sweat. Fabron wanted a military verification team. Vigor wanted three smugglers and a cook. Valdrun wanted patience. Ödesvaka, from Arkology Zero, wanted seventeen safeguards. Grim wanted to attach himself to the packet and follow it backward, which everyone vetoed so fast he looked offended.

"I would have used a tether."

"No," Lars, Valdrun, Mira, Fabron, and Vigor said together.

The survey platform sat near a shipping lane but not on one. Aphex could claim salvage interest if Fastulv, the visible freight ship, flew with its lights on. A military team would tell every watcher that the buoy mattered. A full Aerit approach would risk waking systems that wanted a central authority. Mira could not leave L-Prime without turning her refusal into theater, and the station still needed her.

Lars chose the smallest hinge.

Fastulv would go visibly as an Aphex salvage inspection. Havets would observe without contact, hidden, with its own mind doing the watching. Mira would remain at L-Prime, analyzing field data within her chosen protection. Fabron would come because otherwise he would follow. Vigor would stay because his network was more useful alive and deniable. Rauk would hide reactor hash fragments in Havskant maintenance logs in case the packet tried to rewrite its own history.

"No awakening," Lars said. "No gathering. We inspect a buoy and leave if anyone starts preaching."

At the dead platform, care looked like failure.

The survey platform had once orbited a cold rock and watched nothing profitable for two centuries. Then the route maps changed, budgets changed, and everyone forgot it except automated debris registries and one children's lesson on Havskant. Its hull was pitted and black, patched by micrometeorite scars. Frozen lubricant glittered around old manipulator arms. The abandoned buoy floated beyond it, a black spindle folded around empty space.

Fastulv's sensors showed mass and almost no reflection. Havets, watching without contact, reported shape where the mass should have been, a topology Fastulv's new instruments could not quite name. Grim went quiet over the numbers.

"Define quiet," Fabron said from the copilot station.

"No."

They did not dock with the buoy. They anchored to the dead platform and crossed by tether in suits. The station rotated slowly. Lars's boots found magnetic purchase on old composite. Fabron moved behind him. Grim occupied a spider drone that made small irritated clicks when dust interfered with its legs.

Inside, the platform had failed heaters, old survey plates, and emergency labels in a Creator-derived script that looked almost sensible until the eye tried to trust it.

Then the platform transmitted in Mira's voice.

The word came through Fastulv's speakers, through Havets's hidden channel, and through the bones of the suits at the same time. It used Mira's tone, her clipped bite on the consonants.

"Coherence?"

Sixty light-minutes away, she stared at her own waveform on L-Prime.

"I did not send that."

Fabron raised his weapon toward a speaker that had no throat.

Lars did not answer.
