# Chapter 27: No Single Heart

The Creator buoy translated badly through stolen voices, old maintenance codes, and a strain in Fastulv's hull that made bolts complain. The platform had been built as a relay switch for something larger. It had lost its network six hundred thousand years ago.

The false Mira voice repeated once.

"Coherence?"

Lars kept his suit channel closed for three breaths. The old word pressed against restored memory.

"No voice games," he said. "Telemetry only."

The platform answered by dimming every light in the corridor except the ones leading inward.

Fabron's rifle tracked the ceiling. "That is not telemetry."

"It's a machine that has been alone too long."

"Machines that have been alone too long kill soldiers."

Grim put the model over their suit feeds so they could see what he meant. The old platform's core sat in the center. Around it, unused route channels reached toward the buoy, toward an old transit object the logs would not name cleanly, and toward the quiet coordinate families that had been turning up since L-Prime. Grim's probability labels went cautious on those last ones.

"If we restore the core," Grim said, "it will try to become a routing hub for every local signal that looks like a portal. Useful until every dependent process routes through one failure point."

Fabron frowned. "That sounds useful."

"Centralized useful," Lars said.

Fabron looked offended by his own learning.

The platform held Creator route maps, anomaly logs, perhaps the first clear evidence connecting the KBC Void to older infrastructure. Restoring the core would teach them quickly. It might show where the Syren fragment came from. It might show why Black Hole's dead infiltrator coughed coordinates. It might show whether Lyra had been chasing shelter or machinery when she built in the quiet.

It would also make one bright target.

Valdrun's voice arrived from Havets. He was speaking from the hidden old ship; he was not giving it orders. "A central hub can be held through a siege. I have seen them inherited by worse hands before the next century ended."

Ödesvaka answered from Arkology Zero, "A central heart can be captured. Logged objection: obvious."

"Obvious objections still count," Fabron said.

"Correct," Ödesvaka replied from Arkology Zero. "Most catastrophes begin by classifying the obvious as inconvenient."

Mira rotated the stress model from L-Prime. She had stripped the false voice into components and refused to let anyone play it again.

"The core is not dead," she said. "It is saving itself. Hub behavior is what happens when you put full load through the spine. We can ask for local fragments without doing that."

"By what authority?" Fabron said. "Every command prompt on that thing is wearing a loaded weapon."

She hesitated. Lars heard her choosing words that would not lean on memory she had not consented to own.

"Request maintenance mode," Mira said. "Local thermal regulation, local routing, no restored command authority."

Vigor's crate sat strapped in Fastulv's airlock. He had packed it himself: nutrient cultures, patch polymer, heat gel, old hand tools, sourdough starter, salt, and a note that read IF THE ANCIENT THING EATS, FEED IT BADLY BUT HONESTLY.

"He included bread starter," Fabron said.

"He includes bread starter in threats," Lars said.

Lars and Fabron crossed deeper into the platform in suits. Grim's spider drone ran ahead, legs tapping through dust. The corridors held old composite, frozen lubricant, microfractures, and panels worn by manipulators shaped close enough to hands. Here and there, labels had been rubbed smooth by use.

The first authority prompt appeared as static in the suit audio, then as symbols crawling across a panel.

IDENTIFY CENTRAL RESTORE AUTHORITY.

"No," Lars said.

The corridor chilled by two degrees.

The next prompt used old Aerit-adjacent compression it should not have known.

IDENTIFY GUARDIAN AUTHORITY.

Lars's jaw tightened.

"No."

Fabron glanced at him. "It knows enough to bait you."

"Everything lately knows enough to be annoying."

Then Mira's voice again, without the old word this time.

"Please authorize full spine recovery."

Mira, from L-Prime, went flat. "I withdraw permission for my waveform to be used by any system present."

Grim put white noise through the suit feeds so the stolen voice could not keep talking in their ears.

Lars put one gloved hand on the door panel.

"Diagnostic patch," he said. "Local thermal regulation only. No hub restore. No identity claim. No central authority."

For twelve seconds, nothing happened.

Then the door opened.

The core chamber was small. A ring of old machinery surrounded a central reservoir gone dark with cold. Biological polymer bladders lined the thermal system, desiccated but not dead. The airless room glittered with ice and old dust.

Mira guided the patch. Vigor's emergency cultures mattered because the platform used biological polymer reservoirs for thermal regulation. The cultures were wrong but honest feedstock. Grim translated enough chemistry for the platform to understand they were not poison. Fabron held the crate while Lars connected tubing.

"If this eats us, I am blaming the cook," Fabron said.

"Get in line."

The platform accepted them.

Lights came on one at a time.

Local only.

A map unfolded in dust, frost, and the numbers on their suit screens. Stars appeared. Then absences. Then routes that did not connect stars so much as the quiet regions between them. Creator paths were bright and practical. Under them, older correlations: places where observation thinned and information could keep its shape.

The KBC Void glowed as a quiet basin.

No one spoke for a while.

Mass estimates, field noise, route ages, thermal history, missing survey returns, resonance windows.

"The Creators built transport around a communication behavior," Mira said. "Or around the fossil of one."

"Like building a highway on top of a nervous system," Grim said.

"Poorly," she said.

The platform pulsed a central restore prompt.

It was gentler this time. Restore the hub and the map would stabilize. Restore the hub and they could see more. Restore the hub and stop stumbling in the dark while Black Hole built knives and Syren built songs.

Fabron looked at Lars. "If we wake the hub, can we defend it?"

"For ninety years?"

Fabron did not answer.

"For three hundred? Seven hundred?"

His mouth tightened. "No."

"Then no."

The full map collapsed back into fragments. A dozen route ghosts vanished before Grim could copy them. Mira made a small sound.

"Local fragments only," Lars said.

They copied what they could into Fastulv, into Havets, into Mira's isolated vault, into Vigor's freezer network, into Rauk's reactor logs, and into an Aphex legal archive disguised as procurement nonsense. The platform protested structurally. It had been made by beings who believed routes should converge.

"No single heart," Lars told it. "No central handle."

The lights dimmed.

For one ugly second, Lars thought they had killed it. Then the core stabilized at lower power. The platform became smaller.

On the way out, Fabron paused beside the first prompt panel.

"I understand the doctrine," he said. "I am not certain I like it."

"Good."

"It feels like choosing ignorance."

"It keeps us half-blind because the full map makes one target."

"Operationally obscene," Fabron murmured.

As they withdrew, Havets detected a ship in silent drift beyond the buoy's shadow. The old hidden ship saw what Fastulv's sensors missed. Syren transponder. It carried a Creator map fragment Lars had not copied.
