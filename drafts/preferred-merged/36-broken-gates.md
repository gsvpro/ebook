# Chapter 36: Broken Gates

The dead Creator gate had no tunnel and no platform.

Havets Sista Sång approached the first fragment slowly, at a fraction of what the old ship could do. It did not enter a road. It negotiated with a folded transit node that had once forced two inconvenient places to behave as if they were next to each other, briefly and locally. Fastulv, the new freight ship, followed in ordinary space where it could, and dropped out of the picture whenever the folded geometry stopped respecting normal travel. Fabron hated that. He wrote three reports, deleted all of them, and titled the fourth Unusual Salvage Transit Conditions.

Lars looked at the title and said it was four words of cowardice. Fabron kept writing.

They found the fragment in a cometary graveyard. Dead rings hung among frozen bodies, each cracked at the same angle. Mira put the load on the cracks from L-Prime. The numbers matched fatigue.

The place felt cold even through armor and hull. Abandoned infrastructure still remembering work.

Mira guided the survey. "Passive only. No energizing, no synchronization, no hidden load. I want the gate below response threshold."

Grim sent passive pings so delicate they were almost apologies. Surface-state requests, timed through separate instruments so no single system asked too loudly. The fragment answered with logs.

Creator crews had called the network a triumph. They moved ships through forced adjacency, then cities, then fleets. They mapped quiet basins as low-loss transit conditions. They saw older anchors as stabilizers, natural or abandoned. They built rings, spindles, and folded nodes over them, through them, around them.

Then the failures. Ships arrived with crews remembering conversations they had never had. Cargo holds gained or lost grams of unstable nanomaterial. Travelers reported temporary bodies in sealed compartments, dissolving before anyone could measure them. Medical officers found stress hormones matching fear without an event. Operators called them ghosts. Engineers called them cross-layer contamination. Priests called them summons.

"They documented the same failure in doctrine," Fabron said.

"Everyone does," Lars said. "People find bad infrastructure and recruit it."

They went on to another fragment that had been quieted on purpose. Control systems intact, locally severed, each subsystem holding a shard of function without letting any unit become a hub. Older than Återviens, or something that had taught the same lesson.

Valdrun's voice shook. "She reached this threshold. Or its shadow."

"Maybe," Lars said. "Pluto liked handing her bad old echoes."

Behind a door that opened only when Havets stopped trying to authenticate and sent a tool inventory instead, they found a Creator faction's maintenance cache. Repair plates, broken coupling rings, failed biological interface pods, and memory glass wrapped in ordinary insulation. The crate was marked WASTE PUMPS.

Vigor approved from three systems away. "Sensible people."

"Frightened people," Mira said. "But sometimes that improves labeling."

The glass held a few lines: We mistook reply for road. We increased brightness. The field withdrew. Quiet regions are not empty. They are agreements.

"Useful evidence wrapped in poetry," Mira said. "That makes the tolerance worse, not better."

The fragment offered one active test: an unmanned micro-transit, low mass, through a crippled fold. Lars agreed only after three veto paths and local abort authority held separately by Mira at L-Prime, Fabron aboard Havets, and Ödesvaka at Arkology Zero.

They sent a probe the size of a breadbox. It vanished. It returned eleven seconds later with frost on one side, dust from no local source, a mass increase of 0.7 grams, corrupted anchor telemetry, and a recording of Lars's own voice saying, "Do not make me the answer," captured three minutes before he said it.

Fabron turned slowly. "Time anomalies are an unacceptable reporting category."

"Put that in the report."

Mira analyzed the probe for six hours from L-Prime. Her voice got flatter as the model improved, which was how Lars knew she was either excited or furious.

"The communication layer is loading nearby possibilities into matter systems that were not built for that load," she said. "The probe coupled to a nearby state where your sentence already existed as a field pattern. Consciousness projection could ride that if coupled to prepared substrate. Bad models call it prophecy or ghosts."

"And a good model?"

"Says we are underqualified."

The broken gate map ended at a sealed node the Creators had named First Door. Under that, older absence marked it with a warning that translated only when they dimmed the lights and stopped active scanning: not first.

Lars looked into the dark. Wonder arrived like vertigo. Measurable.

The probe sat in quarantine, shedding its extra 0.7 grams in residue so fine it moved against the air current. Grim measured every particle until the last one decayed into ordinary chemistry.

"Mass error," he said.

"Meaning?"

"The matter arrived with a bad explanation and a lot of confidence."

Fabron closed his report with a line he would later deny writing.

Ancient infrastructure remains active, offended, and badly documented.
