# Chapter 29: Black Hole Nursery

The nursery was hidden inside a hospital ship that had never treated a patient.

Its registry claimed disaster relief. The name on the hull was Mercy of Twelve Harbors, painted in blue letters beside a faded emblem of open hands. The docking lights blinked in the soft pattern medical ships used around frightened refugees. Its public beacon offered pediatric trauma care, epidemic isolation, and emergency organ synthesis.

Havets Sista Sång found it behind a debris moon where the Syren parley route crossed a dead logistics lane. The old hidden ship saw what Fastulv's sensors would have announced. Fastulv stayed three light-minutes out, loud on civilian salvage bands, the new freight hull doing what Lars had built it to do. Fabron's Aphex team approached in a maintenance skiff wearing stolen sanitation credentials. Vigor's smugglers drifted closer under the thermal shadow of an ice hauler and complained over open comms about spoiled onions. Rauk's reactor dampers sat in two black cases under Lars's boots.

The hospital ship's outer lock smelled clean.

No rot. No gore in the corridors. No screaming. Antiseptic air, polished grab rails, children's murals projected on bulkheads, and directional signs pointing toward TRIAGE, FAMILY REUNION, HYDRATION, COUNSELING. The walls were warm to the touch.

Below the public decks, past a locked personnel lift and three false quarantine seals, the cryo bays held no refugees.

They held growth racks, marrow vats, neural scaffolds, refrigerated blood, and failed interface bodies sorted by anomaly probability. Black Hole B-7 had learned to farm what it could not understand.

Lars lay in the observation duct above the main chamber. Old anger arrived without the old neurological lock. Pluto cold. Heliostat hunger. Lyra's hands shaking over a body they could not save. The argument that survival did not justify becoming the thing that hunted you.

Fabron lay beside him in infiltration armor. His breathing was controlled through the helmet link. The main chamber below them reflected in his visor: rows of donor bays, transparent tubing, technicians in white suits with black wrist implants, and a central vat where something small floated in golden nutrient gel.

Grim occupied the ship's maintenance systems.

"Twenty-six living donors," Grim said. "Most ordinary by Aerit standards. Some resilient in ways Black Hole overvalues. One possible locked Aerit. Consent unavailable."

He paused.

"Additional category uncertain."

Lars knew what he meant. The central vat.

"Later," Lars said.

"Later may become a debt."

"Everything in this room is debt."

Fabron shifted enough for armor fabric to whisper. "If we do not wake them, they may die. That is the operational fact."

"If we force it, we may kill who they are trying to remain."

"A hard line in a slaughterhouse."

"Yeah. That is why the boundary matters here."

Fabron looked down through the duct grille. A donor in Bay Fourteen twitched under sedation. A Black Hole technician adjusted the drip without looking at the face.

"I am responsible for every civilian I fail to extract," Fabron said.

"Yes."

"And for every one I turn into evidence by extracting badly."

"Also yes."

"Your doctrine is inconvenient."

"Today it is an invasive step we do not take unless it helps the patient."

Mira fed structural weak points from L-Prime. She had refused to watch the visual feed until they needed her eyes. That refusal lasted eleven minutes. Then she opened one window, saw the vat labels, and went silent long enough for Lars to worry.

"I can help break the ship," she said finally. "Do not ask me to classify the people unless classification changes what you do."

"Understood."

"I mean that exactly. If the answer will not change a clamp, route, or drug, leave it out."

Grim said, "Boundary logged."

They moved.

Fabron's soldiers breached under a sanitation alert Grim had upgraded into a biohazard panic. The ship believed in its own cover. Doors sealed outward. Internal med drones rushed toward false contamination zones. Vigor's smugglers cut external clamps and attached rescue blisters to three maintenance ports. Rauk's dampers made the power spine stutter, never failing, only missing rhythm enough to break central timing.

Lars entered through the ceiling.

He landed on a surgical track above the donor bays and did not use Ragnarrök. The room had vats, oxygen lines, and frightened bodies. The Black Hole surgeon wore human skin. Its white gloves were clean. Its badge said DR. ELLIAN VALE, PEDIATRIC RECONSTRUCTION. Its eyes looked at Lars as a variable.

It did not talk.

Neither did Lars.

He broke its arm, pinned it to a rack, and let Fabron foam it alive for interrogation. The foam hardened around its jaw before it could bite down on the implant behind its molars. Fabron's hands were efficient. His face was not.

"Alive," Fabron told his nearest corporal. "We need buyers, routes, command keys. If it dies without permission, I will be personally disappointed in everyone."

"Major," the corporal said, voice shaking, "yes."

A swarm came out of the ceiling vents, so fine the eye wanted to call it dust. It drifted toward the donor bays. Grim tried to name it and the names would not stay still: sampler, sterilizer, archive denial, tissue tagger. It was designed to decide what the room was while entering lungs.

Havets Sista Sång did not fire a gun. There was nothing to see, no beam, no flash. The old ship reached into the swarm's timing. The programs that were supposed to keep the particles moving together began to give opposite orders. Particles missed the same beat. They fell like soot into containment fields that had not existed in the ship's logs five seconds earlier.

Grim made a sound Lars had never heard from him.

"I cannot hold a clean label," Grim said. "These are hostile experiments with incomplete names."

"Answer no," Lars said.

The rescue became labor.

Awkward, slow, dangerous work. Fabron's soldiers cut restraints and transferred bodies into rescue blisters while med alarms lied at them. Vigor's smugglers kept count in kitchen shorthand: six warm, eight breathing assisted, two pressure unstable, one needs hands now. Rauk's dampers kept the ship from locking the bays. Mira found which tubes could be cut and which had to be capped.

"Bay Seven cap blue, cut green. Do not touch silver. Silver is spinal."

"Bay Nine has two feeds labeled nutrition. One is compliance drug. Cut the left by temperature, not color."

"Bay Twelve, wait."

Bay Twelve held Isaac Tam.

Broad-faced. Farmer's hands. A labor tattoo a century out of date on his inner wrist. His file called him forty-nine. His bone density said two hundred. Old fractures had healed too cleanly. His bloodwork carried repair signatures under ordinary human noise. His neural lock patterns held even under sedation.

Black Hole's file marked him: PROBABLE OLD-SOURCE HOST. OPENING RISK: HIGH. HARVEST PRIORITY: PRESERVE.

Lars wanted to tear the ship apart with his bare hands.

Instead, he breathed.

"Mira," he said, "do we move him without waking him?"

"Physical risk moderate. Cognitive risk lower if no identity language, no old greeting, no direct contradiction of cover memory. Keep evidence minimal. Ask for movement only."

Fabron's voice cut in. "He is sedated."

"Sedation is not consent," Mira said. "It may still permit a motor response if he is near surface. Do not turn my sentence into permission for everything."

"Understood," Fabron said.

Grim projected a simple message into the bay audio, low and plain.

You are in danger. We can move your body without unlocking memory. We will not open locked identity without consent. Signal if you understand.

For seven seconds nothing happened.

The room fought around them. A soldier swore. A donor coughed blood into a mask. Somewhere below, a Black Hole technician tried to vent Bay Three and died when Fabron's rear team stopped him. Lars kept his eyes on Isaac's hand.

Then Isaac's finger tapped once.

Yes to movement.

"Move him," Lars said.

Fabron's team moved him carefully. No one said Aerit. No one said rescue. They kept his file closed, his face covered from cameras, his hand free in case he needed to withdraw even that narrow permission.

They took him, twenty-five others, and records enough to make Black Hole buyers nervous for decades.

They did not save everything.

Four cloned tissue lines self-sterilized when the ship realized extraction was failing. Grim tried to freeze the process and could not. The tissue curled inward. A donor in Bay Nineteen survived because a smuggler named Jessa held a manual airway open for nine minutes while crying hard enough to fog her face shield. Two Black Hole technicians chose explosive implant death over capture and took a section of archive with them.

Then the central vat opened its eyes.

The body inside was child-sized because Black Hole had chosen that growth path. Limbs too thin. Skull shaped by committee. Skin translucent over scaffolds. Something under the sternum made Lars's implanted instincts recoil.

It looked at him.

It asked, in no language Lars knew, whether it had been made wrong.

The question arrived through field vibration, vat compression, and the part of the room that older machines used for almost-meaning. Grim tried to categorize it and failed.

"I do not have a name that holds," Grim said. "The substrate is moving. The face is looking. The probability distribution is unacceptable."

Fabron stood at the vat edge with his weapon lowered. "Can it feel pain?"

Mira's answer came shaking. "Define pain."

"Doctor."

"Do not rush me because the room is horrible."

No one did.

Mira pulled data from the vat, not the face. Growth logs, neural continuity scans, substrate load, fragment behavior, sensory integration, memory loops.

"It is not a person yet," she said at last. "The substrate has no stable continuity. Failed interface around stolen fragments. There are reflexive distress loops and borrowed response patterns. Termination may be mercy. Do not call it cleanup."

Lars wanted someone else to decide.

"Options," he said.

"Immediate termination stops distress and destroys evidence," Mira said. "Continued operation prolongs distress and risks Black Hole recovery behavior. Sedation and cryogenic ethical hold preserve evidence and prevent further loop reinforcement. It does not solve the question. It only refuses to lie about solving it."

Fabron closed his eyes once. "Then we carry the debt."

"Yes," Mira said.

So they sedated it, froze it, and marked it as an ethical grave for later.

Lars stayed until the cryo light turned blue. He did not know what prayer would be appropriate, so he checked the clamps twice.

The archive unlocked under duress as the ship lost power spine authority. Its doctrine files were worse than the rooms because they had no smell and no trembling hands. Clean diagrams and ambitions.

The files said Aerit blood could be used as a key. They said Creator portals were gates. They said the KBC Void was a throne, and the Nomad field a godhead to be consumed.

Black Hole did not understand the science. It understood appetite. It believed enough harvested anomaly, enough forced continuity, enough stolen route maps would let it sit in the quiet basin and command whatever older field the Creators had misunderstood.

"They think scale means ownership," Grim said.

"Predators usually do," Lars said.

Fastulv pulled away under false salvage distress while Havets erased the routes that mattered. The old ship did not fire. It changed the navigation records so some paths vanished and others would lead Black Hole's competitors into legal trouble. Fabron's soldiers sat with rescued donors in the blisters, helmets off when safe, hands visible when people woke confused. Jessa refused to stop holding Bay Nineteen's hand until med took over. Rauk's dampers burned out in a neat row.

Isaac slept in a protected compartment.

His consent record displayed one line.

Move me. Do not open me.

Lars stared at it longer than necessary.

Fabron came to stand beside him. The major had blood on one sleeve and foam residue on the other.

"We left records behind," Fabron said.

"Some."

"We left bodies."

"Yes."

"We froze a question in a box."

"Yes."

Fabron swallowed. "I will account for my part."

"Don't make it a speech."

"It is a responsibility."

Mira joined the channel without video. "The donors need independent advocates before debrief. No Aphex custody language. No anomaly labels in medical headers. Isaac's boundary line should be visible to anyone who enters his room."

"Done," Lars said.

"And the vat file?"

"Ethical grave. Locked review. No owner."

Her breath shook once over the channel. "Good. Not enough, but good."

Lars sent Isaac's line to every trusted channel.
