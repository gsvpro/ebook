# Chapter 33: Coherence Break

Emergency red came back in pulses. The observatory shook. Dust drifted from the cupola ribs. Somewhere below, an air-recycler screamed, failed, and came back on with a wet cough.

Fabron was on the soldier channel, counting who still answered.

Mira had the ring data on a crooked camera. One cheek was smeared with dust.

Grim had too many systems open. The list would not stay still.

Mother Pell had both hands on the broadcast controls and a convert's bright look on her face.

Lars asked Havets Sista Sång to shut Door Nine, blind Black Hole, silence Syren, keep the civilians, keep the evidence, keep Mira's skull intact, and make the rest of them understand they had been idiots. The old ship did not take it. Grim could only watch it refuse a list that large.

One word came back through the hidden layer.

Smaller.

Door Nine still held a partial charge. Three Black Hole ships were caught in the ring's pull. They could not go in and they could not get out. Their engines poured energy into the ring. Syren's array put a shared rhythm into the same pull, under the prayer. The station shook with all of it.

Grim threw the telemetry across Lars's vision. Ship heat. Ring charge. Civilian locations. Broadcast harmonics. Black Hole processors spiking as stolen flesh was forced to run like equipment.

"They are running the engines on biological processors," Grim said. "Stolen living tissue, used as computers. You can see the heat. The control programs are fighting each other. There is no consent in the logs."

"Can it break the ring?"

"It may take the shared field as a valid input and push harder."

Valdrun came on the channel, hard with fear. "Wake nearby sleepers. We need more minds. I have watched stations die while we kept our hands clean."

Ödesvaka objected at once from Arkology Zero. "Consent unavailable for two candidates in range. Emergency breach request denied pending authorization impossible to obtain."

"Then we lose the station."

"Then we do not commit breach and call it rescue."

People were still bleeding while they argued.

Mira cut through. Her camera was crooked. Behind her, L-Prime's emergency board painted red bars across the lab walls.

"I can model the ring if I use lock-adjacent pattern recognition. Not memory work. Stress analysis. My choice."

"Risk?" Lars asked. "Give me the ugly number."

"Overload. Involuntary recall. Possible me yelling at you. Failure mode: identity shear."

"Baseline."

"Consent given for analysis only. Record it."

Grim recorded. Ödesvaka stamped the boundary into three separate logs from Arkology Zero. Havets took the shape because it was small enough to run.

Mira opened the structure. Identity stayed locked. Door Nine sat under her hands as fatigue. Her pupils dilated. Her jaw clenched. Two monitors behind her lost sync as the analysis rig borrowed pattern capacity the lock still guarded.

"No memory prompt," she said through her teeth.

"No memory prompt," Lars said.

"If I start naming corridors, cut the feed."

"Done."

The Creator ring had been repaired toward transport, again and again. Each repair had pushed it further that way. Underneath, an older layer did not move bodies. It linked local minds across quiet gaps. It offered coherence without a command. The Creators had used it as a road. Then they built more roads.

"The load is too even," Mira said. "Stop feeding it the same phase. You are packing the ring like a pressure vessel."

Fabron, fighting through the cupola, barked, "How do we break that without losing the station?"

"Break the shared timing. Give every local system its own phase. Make the load ugly and local."

Valdrun still wanted the sleepers opened. Guardian Channel Two backed him too quickly, with a clean recommendation that had no fear in it. Ödesvaka threatened lockout from Arkology. Fabron wanted a decision. Pell screamed that silence had chosen them. Black Hole dumped more processors into the engine loops.

Lars snapped at Valdrun. Valdrun snapped back with centuries in his teeth. Grim put up three options, assigned probabilities, and got angry when Lars rejected all of them because none of them kept Mira from being spent like a spare part. Mira said old people were not automatically wise; they were old load-bearing members with hidden fractures. Pell called them cowards. Ödesvaka logged Pell's statement from Arkology Zero as "hostile theological interference," which did not help.

Fabron told everyone to shut up because soldiers were dying while they invented paperwork.

That worked.

Lars laughed once, ugly and relieved.

"Fine. No single answer. Fabron, evacuate by local cells. No group bigger than twelve. Grim, desynchronize every speaker and suit comm. Mira, give me phase windows and stop before your brain gives out. Valdrun, shield sleepers but do not open them. Ödesvaka, enforce the line. Havets, local safeguards."

The old ship answered.

Coherence.

The room moved because the decision had edges.

Grim shattered the Syren song into nonsense. Every corridor got a different phase: broken chimes, maintenance alerts, recipes from Vigor's kitchen network, Fabron's own clipped evacuation orders staggered by milliseconds until the shared timing could not find a spine. People heard different things in different rooms, and the song lost its hold. Fabron moved civilians in groups too small for the field to use, mixed soldiers and converts so no one rhythm could run a cluster.

Mira fed phase windows in numbers so bare they sounded like pain.

"Three seconds. Node two. Not the ring. The bracket. The bracket is carrying the false load."

Lars fired the Ragnarrök not at Door Nine but at three structural nodes holding the broadcast array in alignment.

The array collapsed crookedly.

Door Nine dimmed.

A Black Hole ship pulled free, engines dumping blue-white heat into the dark.

The next hull stayed caught in the ring's pull. Its stolen processors locked onto the wrong job, the bracket instead of the engines, and could not let go. The living hull tore away from the metal frame underneath it. The wreck tumbled, leaking air and coolant, and then it came apart.

On Mira's lab screen a contact went into the ring and left a smear of leftover mass. Grim went quiet. The smear sat in a crescent. The books would not accept it as local manufacture.

Mira disconnected and vomited off camera.

No one called it awakening.

Afterward the alarms were still on, only less desperate. Valdrun sent Lars a private channel.

"I would have opened them."

"I know."

"I might still have been right."

Lars looked at the station damage map, at the saved cells and the dead ones, at Mira's blank medical status waiting for a human update.

"I know that too."

Neither of them apologized. Not yet. The disagreement stayed in the room.
