## Chapter 13: The Coherence Plan

After Fastulv had settled into its internal berth the planning chamber unfolded the old command architecture of Arkology Zero. The floor darkened to a black mirror and the Milky Way rose out of it in layered light: trade corridors, war zones, silent colonies, hidden habitats, energy monopolies, slave routes dressed up as labor logistics, artifact markets, religious splinters, private fleets, and old Aerit nodes, each in its own spectrum. Lars could see too many layers at once. For the first time since waking he did not flinch from any of them. He simply wished he had more coffee.

Valdrun had changed clothes, a dark coat, fitted utility layers, boots that had seen a lot of use. The integrated structures along his neck and left side were partly covered now, but not hidden. He looked less like a rescued casualty and more like someone who had been waiting a very long time and was ready to get to work.

"This is why we do not improvise," Valdrun said.

"We improvise constantly."

"No. We adapt within prepared constraints."

Grim stood inside three overlapping interface rings, one connected to Station Zero, one to Fastulv, and one to Havets Sista Sång as a guest, because the old ship had its own mind and did not take orders from a brain that was barely a year old. Grim's body stayed still, but Lars could feel the attention spreading through the systems around them.

"I have organized the action model," Grim said.

The board showed five jobs. First they had to hide: keep anyone from finding Arkology Zero, and keep anyone from tying Fastulv's turn into the Void to missing spacetime. Then they had to repair the auxiliary chamber, the guardian channels, the medical gear, and the communication filters. Valdrun closed his eyes briefly and said he supported the wording less than the goal. Then they had to make contact, Vigor first, then the guardian channels that could be reached without exposing the network, then the priority nine likely Aerits under threat. The blurred list appeared again. Lars did not reach for it yet. After that came a story for Black Hole B-7, Aphex, the galactic police, and every opportunist with a sensor array, plausible and wrong. Intervention would stay limited and local. They would strengthen nodes, remove predators where necessary, and seed better failure behavior.

Lars looked at the galaxy model when Grim said failure behavior. Grim highlighted a failing habitat that ran on a single NUKEA-derived reactor stack a corporate governor had turned into a proprietary dependency. The board also showed a mining cluster where life support was tied to labor compliance, and a religious enclave near old Syren space with high weapons density.

"Systems fail," Grim said. "The question is whether they fail cleanly, locally, and recoverably, or whether they drag everything connected to them into the hole."

Valdrun nodded. Återviens did not stop every collapse, he said. It made collapse survivable.

Ödesvaka hovered over the map, turning slowly, and requested a long-range plan. Lars asked who wanted it. The drone said he did, recovered command habit, and logged whatever he muttered at it.

The station dimmed several of the immediate tactical layers and expanded time, ten years, ninety, three hundred, seven hundred, and the galaxy changed under each projection. Some red regions burned out quickly. Others matured into governments. Green hidden nodes survived by remaining small, then failed when descendants got ambitious. Blue Aerit traces flickered, went dark, and reappeared under other names. Aphex Defence either became a stabilizing force in several branches or a private empire in three ugly ones. NUKEA standards either kept colonies alive through modular power independence or became licensing chains that strangled poorer systems.

Lars watched nine decades pass in twelve seconds. It felt normal. That was new.

"We think too short when we are scared," Valdrun said. "Even we do. The first plan must work tomorrow, but it must not poison the next century."

Lars thought about that while he looked at the model. Anything they did would leak, not the details perhaps, but the patterns, and people copied patterns. If they used giant heroic interventions someone would build cults around them. If they solved every problem with superior force, the lesson became force. If they centralized the rescue effort, the lesson became centralization. So the interventions had to look local and a bit boring, like maintenance, trade disputes, insurance audits, reactor recalls, food-chain independence grants, embarrassing leaks, small militias deciding not to be cowards. They should let people think they had saved themselves wherever possible.

Valdrun's expression went still. He was listening.

Grim said quietly that this was in Lyra's late doctrine. Lars had assumed as much.

The planning chamber accepted the principle and the model shifted, replacing several direct intervention arrows with softer influence loops: anonymous technical fixes, cultural seeding, targeted exposure of predatory actors, hidden evacuation routes, quiet restoration of local manufacturing, and communication protocols disguised as ordinary trade redundancy.

"We need names," Lars said. "Not for the whole thing. For the first operation."

Grim said operation names were often counterproductive. Lars shrugged and wanted one anyway.

Valdrun looked at the first cluster: The Rim, Aphex activity, Black Hole B-7 debris, Vigor's farm, Fastulv's false trail, and a likely Black Hole relay hidden near a derelict ice hauler two light-hours outward. First task was to cut the trail. If Black Hole B-7 had a passive relay on the departure cone it might not locate them, but it could sell uncertainty, and uncertainty attracted hunters. So they would remove the relay, quietly.

Grim enlarged the relay estimate. The object was shielded, cold, and probably disguised as salvage. Destroying it with Fastulv would be easy and obvious. Anyone watching The Rim would see the new freight ship's engines. Havets Sista Sång could approach through the folded edge and erase it without a conventional vector. The old ship would not show.

The old ship's icon glowed beside Fastulv. Lars looked at it. Havets Sista Sång had been awake for less than a day, after three hundred and fifteen years of waiting under his command envelope. It had greeted him like a captain. It had not yet moved.

"We use her," Lars said.

Valdrun nodded once. Grim's lenses brightened. Ödesvaka rotated and said ancient vessel activation required command confirmation, medical stability confirmation, and risk acceptance.

"Medical stability?" Lars asked.

"Eighty-one percent."

"Plenty."

Ödesvaka said that was disputed, by the medical system, guest entity Grim, guardian Valdrun, Havets Sista Sång, and this unit. Grim added that a backup plan would not hurt.

"Fine. We make it a field test. Short hop. Cut the relay. Leave a false signature leading somewhere useless."

Valdrun asked where. Lars looked at the map, a dead cometary mining claim, a collapsed influencer retreat, a derelict luxury habitat once owned by a tax-avoidance cult, and pointed.

Grim checked the tag. The former executive wellness moon of Helix Prosperity Church.

Ödesvaka's blue symbols flickered and asked for an operation designation.

"Quiet Dignity."

Valdrun looked pained. Grim said Operation Quiet Dignity logged, and the chamber accepted it.
