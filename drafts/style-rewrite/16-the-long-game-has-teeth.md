## Chapter 16: The Long Game Has Teeth

Gabriel Arsenault answered on the third encrypted layer. The projection appeared above the planning table in Arkology Zero with a three-second delay and enough encryption noise wrapped around it to make a civilian comm system melt. Arsenault looked immaculate.

"Monsieur Odin," Arsenault said. "You are difficult to locate."

"Thank you."

Arsenault's eyes flicked to the background. Lars had set the projection field to show only a bland cabin wall from Fastulv, slightly out of focus. Fastulv was the new freight ship, the one Arsenault already knew about. No Station Zero. No Valdrun. No Grim unless he chose to lean in. Grim stayed just outside the frame.

"Black Hole B-7 attempted to kill me," Lars said. "Then they tried to watch my departure. Then they deployed hardware consistent with biological acquisition."

Arsenault's expression lost its theatrical polish. He asked what that meant. A blood sampler, Lars said, with cryo-sealant and a transmission seed, an ugly little thing. Did Lars have the device? Parts, Lars said. Give them to Aphex. No. Monsieur Odin. Gabriel. The chairman stopped. Lars let the silence sit.

He had spent decades, apparently centuries, hiding what he was. He had also spent a few years building a public identity that made powerful people underestimate him until money or plasma corrected them. Arsenault was vain, and French in the theatrical sense, and he understood tone.

"What do you need?" Arsenault asked.

"A security audit."

"Of what?"

"Aphex research dependencies with unusual biological, cybernetic, or longevity-related anomalies in personnel records."

Arsenault went still. That was a strange category. So was being attacked by fascists with blood hardware. How broad? Ninety-year retrospective, quiet, no mass alerts, no heroic raids, no executive speeches. Arsenault said Lars wounded him. Lars said he was trying not to.

Arsenault looked away, probably at advisors Lars could not see. Galactic police were already crawling over The Rim. Major Fabron had submitted a report that used the phrase "recklessly effective" seven times, and he wanted to know where Lars was. Tell him I am busy, Lars said, and tell him to secure the recovered Black Hole equipment and stop asking questions he did not have clearance to survive. Arsenault's gaze sharpened. Clearance from whom? Physics.

There was an Aphex dependency, Lars continued, with someone Black Hole might target. He did not know who yet. The audit would create cover for him to find out. Arsenault said he was asking Aphex Defence to help him conduct an undisclosed counteroperation inside their own structure. Yes. This was outrageous. Also cheaper than losing the person and discovering what Black Hole did with them.

Arsenault did not answer immediately. In the planning chamber the real model floated behind the false projection: Aphex nodes, audit pathways, legal triggers, internal security chains, Major Fabron's brigade, Professor Fayolle's lab network, and three possible locations where the priority Aerit might be buried under ordinary employment records. Valdrun watched from the shadows. Grim ran probability branches. Ödesvaka silently logged things Lars would later deny.

Finally Arsenault said he could authorize a strategic integrity audit under post-attack emergency powers. Boring name, good function. It would give Lars access to certain records, and movement cover. And movement cover, Arsenault said reluctantly. No one outside his smallest circle was to hear the biological angle. Agreed. No samples left Aphex custody without Lars's review. Monsieur Odin. Gabriel. Another silence. Agreed.

"Good. Also, if Fabron complains, promote him."

Arsenault blinked. Why? Because he was irritating, brave, and not completely stupid. Arsenault said Lars had an unusual leadership philosophy.

Lars turned back to the real planning table.

"Deception moving."

Grim said the Aphex audit cover was established. Concealment had improved. The false trail to the wellness moon had already been queried by a Black Hole listening post. They were taking the bait cautiously. Valdrun said repair crews were restoring the auxiliary chamber. No robot entered without dumb mechanical fallback. Ödesvaka had filed seventeen complaints about Lars's cable rig, which had saved Valdrun's life and also violated six station maintenance principles.

"Contact?" Lars asked.

Grim brought up Vigor's response channel, then the blurred priority nine list. One entry sharpened a little more under the Aphex audit overlay. Name hidden. Location: Aphex Defence research dependency, Lagrange facility near a cold brown dwarf on the old French expansion line. Public identity: materials fatigue specialist. Age on record: forty-two. Estimated actual age: between five hundred and seven hundred. Threat: Black Hole acquisition probability rising. Memory lock status: active. Consent route: unknown.

Lars stared at the estimate. Forty-two. Five hundred to seven hundred. Somewhere out there another person was walking around with a life, a job, habits, perhaps friends, perhaps a favorite bad pub. They thought they were mortal in the ordinary way. Maybe they had headaches they could not explain. Maybe they fixed impossible problems and called it luck. Maybe they were being watched.

"We go there," Lars said.

Valdrun shook his head. They prepare there. Difference? A great deal. They did not rush in as ancient saviors. They established cover, studied their life, identified immediate threats, and decided whether contact was safe. If they contacted, they asked. Consent constraint. Valdrun's rule. Lars nodded.

Long-term Återviens, Valdrun said, started with not breaking the people they claimed to save. The room accepted that.

Grim opened another layer. Havets Sista Sång, the old ship, could carry them near the brown dwarf without conventional detection. Fastulv should be the visible asset afterward: Lars Odin, Aphex board member and weapons consultant, arriving for an audit in the new freight ship, annoying and plausible. Anyone watching would look at Fastulv. They would not see Havets. Lars said he had been practicing for that for years.

Ödesvaka rotated and asked for a mission designation. Lars considered. They were going to find someone asleep inside their own life. They would walk near Black Hole B-7 nets and Aphex secrets. They would use Havets Sista Sång and Fastulv, Valdrun, Grim, Ödesvaka, and a man who had learned that his life was a cover.

He looked at Valdrun.

"Coherence?"

Valdrun nodded.

"Coherence."

Grim answered too, less hesitant this time.

"Coherence."

Ödesvaka's surface symbols brightened. Mission designation: Hold Coherence. No one objected.

Outside the planning chamber Havets Sista Sång began warming old systems that had not moved in three centuries. In the secondary hangar Fastulv sat under the station lights, full of beef, wine, and illegal parts. The materials specialist in the Aphex records was still at work.

Far away, or perhaps not far at all, a temporary body stood in a locked room for less than two seconds and left behind particles that folded into nothing.
