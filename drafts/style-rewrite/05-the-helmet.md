## Chapter 5: The Helmet

It beeps. Pip pip pip, what can that be? He hears voices, slowly he opens his eyes. An unfamiliar blurred face says incomprehensible things.

He wakes again. Was it a dream? He looks around. He lies in a bed in a white sterile room with apparatuses beside. Slowly the memories begin to come back. What happened? The door opens and Grim comes in, followed by Valdrun.

"We were really worried for a while, but you are starting to come back."

"How long?"

"Forty-eight hours, we had to put you to sleep," said Valdrun.

"Oh, then it must have been serious."

"You can trust that, your nanobots were completely confused."

"Nanobots?" asked Lars, and grimaced when the pain began to spread from the neck up into the head.

"Ah, we will fix that, I have a solution you will like. Besides, you are a true hero who saved me," said Valdrun, and patted him on the shoulder.

Valdrun stood up. He was tall and powerful and looked like a mix between a robot and a Viking, with a somewhat unnatural blue-pale skin like a vampire. Ödesvaka hung half in shadow near the ceiling, symbols moving across the sphere, and said something about medical stability that Lars did not bother to answer.

Lars slurped the strong coffee from the cup and set it down thoughtfully. "Was it the fumes or the radiation that affected me?"

"It was probably both, you were directly exposed to high doses of radiation and gases that rose upward."

"It was pure chance that I survived. If the radiation had had another direction and the gases had not risen and been ventilated out, it would have been over for my part," said Valdrun, and drummed his fingers on the table. "It is starting to sink in now, we could have lost six hundred years of progress, actually more, thousands of years. The lineage could have been cut. We have been too clueless and not thought of all scenarios. We would have needed better redundancy. We are getting slack."

Lars looked up at Valdrun. "Yes, hard words but true. It is easy to be wise after the fact. Luckily we can now improve. Maybe time to run a bunch of AI simulations? Besides, could you not have sent off a ship or a signal?"

"The only ship that would have made the whole distance out of the KBC Void is Havets Sista Sång, the old ship you just walked through, and it was locked to your command. Directed laser would not have worked, too many planets and dust in the way. Radio would not have reached either, it spreads out and disappears after a couple of light-years, and besides it can be perceived by the enemy."

Grim came in with a cup of steaming strong coffee. "Isn't this coffee good. I have run 1892 different scenarios for a timespan of two hundred years forward. There is improvement potential. The station's supercomputer is really useful. But simplest is to plan for the worst and hope for the best."

Valdrun stood up. "All right, eat up, we have an important day ahead."

A device stood in the middle of the room with a seat. Valdrun asked Lars to sit and then folded a helmet down over his head.

"Sit still, do not think about anything, and look at the pattern."

"How is that supposed to work," mumbled Lars.

After five minutes the machine shut off automatically. Valdrun clapped his hands.

"Then that is done, how does it feel?"

"Yes, as usual—" Lars stiffened and went pale in the face when the memories streamed back. Not all of them. Enough. A smell of bread in a station corridor. A hand in his hair. A voice saying a name that was not Lars. He was small, running along a white corridor on this station. Stockholm sat wrong now.

"Sit calmly and breathe deeply, Lars."

"How old am I?"

Valdrun did not look away.

"Six hundred and forty-one. You were born in 2133. The three hundred and fifteen years on Havets Sista Sång was how long since last command access. You believed you were about thirty-five. That was the lock."

The answer passed through Lars like cold water. He sat in the chair and took one breath, then another.

"I need a much stronger coffee."
