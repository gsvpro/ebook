## Chapter 9: Threshold Models

Lunch did not look like Valdrun's glass. It was herring, potatoes, crispbread, cheese, butter, and something Grim called historically suspicious, though he ate it anyway after installing a temporary brine-tolerance patch.

Afterward the barge simulation faded. The water lost depth, the sun softened, the deck became a grid, then white floor, then the clean oval of Simulation Chamber 3. The planning model remained.

Grim had turned Lyra's threshold archives into a layered working board. Each branch showed how sure the source was, how old the data was, and what it recommended. Lars had never liked certainty. Certainty was what people used when they ran out of instruments.

"Open the first file," he said.

Grim enlarged a band of amber indicators. "Hostile interest in carrier biology. Artifact black markets, unexplained disappearances of long-lived individuals, abnormal blood-analysis procurement, and pattern-matched attacks like the one on The Rim. Seventeen strong matches over the last ninety years. Forty-two weak. Three confirmed by guardian-side metadata."

"Confirmed deaths?"

Valdrun looked at Grim. Grim's voice flattened. "Two."

Lars nodded once.

Grim opened the next file. Red filaments appeared between corporate sovereignties, paramilitary zones, collapsed colonies, and religious war networks.

"Civilizational overcomplexity," Grim said.

"Civilizational cancer," Lars said, and the phrase arrived sideways, the same way the napkin answers arrived. Valdrun did not smile this time.

"Systems that cannot simplify keep adding control layers," Valdrun said. "Each layer fixes failures the last one created. Eventually the management structure eats more resilience than it gives back."

Grim highlighted a gas giant megacity in a French-Chinese corporate protectorate. "This habitat ring needs thirty-two energy imports that have to arrive on time, fourteen outside food streams, two proprietary atmosphere engines, and a legal code so large its judicial AI uses a dedicated moon."

"That sounds expensive."

"It is considered prosperous."

The model showed one broken energy import, then a delayed food stream, then a maintenance strike suppressed by private security, then riots, then emergency control layers, then more dependency. The habitat did not collapse in the simulation. It survived by becoming worse.

Valdrun opened the guardian file. "Not waking all Aerits at once. This authorizes finding specific hidden carriers. They can restore local nodes, repair damaged archives, or protect vulnerable networks."

"How many?"

Grim opened a list and immediately blurred most of it. "Medical lock still protects parts of your memory. But from archive indexing and current threat data: initial priority group is nine."

"Nine Aerits."

"Nine likely Aerits," Valdrun said. "Some may not survive full unlock. Some may refuse. Some may have built lives that cannot be broken open casually."

That landed harder than Lars expected. People had lives, jobs, friends, cover identities that had become real.

"We ask," Lars said. "We do not drag anyone into this because an archive says threshold. If they are in immediate danger, we protect first. But the unlock is consent-based unless they are dying and unable to choose."

Grim tilted his head. "That was in Lyra's later ethical constraints. Your phrasing was less formal."

"Good."

Ödesvaka hovered in from the chamber wall. "Consent constraint marked as captain directive."

"Call it a practical rule."

"Directive downgraded to practical rule."

Valdrun folded his hands behind his back. "There is one more immediate issue."

Grim expanded The Rim's traffic logs. "Aphex has secured the station sector. Galactic police have opened a formal terrorism inquiry. Black Hole B-7 survivors are absent, which means either none survived, or someone extracted them quickly. Fastulv's departure vector is filed, but our correction into the Void is suspicious."

"How suspicious?"

"To a competent investigator, extremely."

"Can we alter the logs?"

"Yes."

Valdrun shook his head. "Do not erase. Erasures attract specialists. Add noise consistent with sensor degradation near the Void, then give Aphex a plausible reason to classify the rest."

"Gabriel Arsenault owes me several billion favors."

"He paid you several billion kronor," Grim said.

"Different column."

Lars walked around the model. No migraine stopped him. No lock punished him. Missing knowledge felt like holes in a map. His thoughts avoided those places not because pain blocked them, but because the road simply ended.

"We send Arsenault a board-level security advisory," Lars said. "Black Hole B-7 attempted acquisition of Aphex strategic assets, including one director and one experimental weapons platform. That gets him to classify, suppress, and overreact in exactly the useful direction. Ragnarrök is the platform."

Grim added the Aphex action line. "What about Vigor?"

"Send him proof of Valdrun recovery. Minimal packet. Guardian authentication, no station position, no detailed medical logs."

Ödesvaka's symbols flickered. "Approved."

"And the priority nine?" Valdrun asked.

Lars looked at the blurred list. "We need the next unlock stage before I touch that list."

Valdrun nodded. "Yes."

"How bad is it?"

"Less physically dangerous than the first. More emotionally difficult. It restores relational memory."

Lars did not ask what that meant. Mother. Father. Lyra. Valdrun. Vigor. Friends. Deaths. His own deaths.

Grim's voice softened. "We can wait."

"No. We do not rush blindly, but we do not wait because I dislike feelings."

Valdrun moved to the chamber wall. A medical route appeared, leading back toward the helmet room. "Then we prepare. Eat first. Hydration. Stabilization scan. Then stage two."

"I just ate fish."

"You need more than fish."

Lars sighed. "Fine."
