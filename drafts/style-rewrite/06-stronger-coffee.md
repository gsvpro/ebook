## Chapter 6: Stronger Coffee

The stronger coffee arrived in a cup that looked too fine for what it contained. Lars took a careful sip and drank some more before he said anything.

Valdrun sat opposite with his long hands around nothing in particular. Grim stood by the wall display, where the station had given him Lars's medical telemetry after Valdrun authorized it and Lars had complained for a while, mostly out of habit. The display showed cellular activity, neural bands, blood chemistry, and several layers Lars did not have names for and did not want names for.

"How much is still locked?" Grim asked.

Valdrun looked at the display. "Most of the life story. The first stage put back the pieces he needs to stand: where he was born, how old he is, the name that answers when someone calls him, that this station is familiar, and enough medical access so his own systems stop fighting the lock."

"So I know who I am, but not what I did."

"Yes."

Valdrun opened a new picture on the wall. It showed the station in layers: hangars, medical rings, housing, workshops, archives, power loops, hidden docks, and the damaged auxiliary chamber now marked amber instead of red.

"This place is Arkology Zero. Station Zero is the old public translation. It sits a few light-years into the KBC Void, but not in ordinary space the way The Rim does. The outer surface you walked on was only the door. The real structure is folded inside a spacetime bubble. That is why people outside have never mapped us, why ordinary signals fail, and why only certain ships can enter without tearing themselves up."

Lars looked at the map. It was too large.

"How many are here?"

Valdrun's face changed, not much, enough.

"Awake, now? Me, Ödesvaka, the station intelligences, and some limited biological cultures in preservation. You and Grim arrived from outside. Most of the housing is empty or shut down for conservation."

"Empty because everyone left?"

"Because everyone hid."

The word hid did not trigger the old migraine. Lars set down the coffee.

"Practical version."

Valdrun nodded. "In the 2180s Lyra started Alpha Fourteen. The goal was survival. Our numbers were too small, our technology too desirable, and the galaxy had begun doing what the galaxy always does when someone finds a lever: every tyrant, oligarch, sect, and military cult wanted to pull it. We made ourselves forget. Everyone except seven guardians stayed exempt so they could keep the hidden work running. The others were sent out into ordinary civilization with layered identities and hidden triggers. They could live, work, adapt, and avoid exposing what they were."

Lars drank coffee. That was better than answering. The medical chair had given him one clean fact and a thousand dirty implications. Six hundred and forty-one. Born on Station Zero. Not from Stockholm. His name had been Laurðinn before it became Lars Odin. He did not feel six hundred and forty-one. He felt tired, hungry, irritated, and possibly under-caffeinated.

"Your current cover was designed to pass a casual check," Valdrun continued. "Birth records, education fragments, social traces, money trails, medical records. Stockholm was useful because your surface accent and habits already leaned Swedish. You were raised with several languages here. Galactic Swedish became dominant later. You remember Stockholm. You remember connective tissue that was put in. Not all of it is false. Some is drawn from actual stays on Earth and in Swedish habitats. The lock did not simply erase and replace. It compressed, braided, and redirected."

"The seven guardians stayed awake," Valdrun went on. "Not rulers. Keys. They kept the hidden nodes, the systems that can take the lock off, the memory restoration, the emergency activation. We delegated constantly. Moved constantly. Avoided a single command post."

"Who?"

Valdrun hesitated.

"If that hesitation is for my health, I dislike it but approve."

"It is. Names carry memory weight. Some will be easier after the next stage. The ones you have already stepped in: Lyra Vargstjärna. Founder. First carrier of the Pluto line."

The name did not hurt. It rang instead, far away. Grim's lenses brightened, but he did not interrupt.

"Your mother and father. Also guardians. Their names are not for this stage."

Lars's fingers curled around the cup.

"Alive?"

"Unknown."

Lars nodded once.

"You."

"Me."

"Vigor."

Valdrun's eyebrow lifted.

"He wore a false beard. He had a lot of defensive infrastructure. He gave away Xaltor X."

"Vigor Hallman is one of the seven."

"And two more?"

"Later."

"Fine."

Grim enlarged part of the station map. "Why did Vigor not come here if he is awake?"

"He likely did not know I was trapped. Station Zero's outgoing messages under watch-state are extremely restricted. After the chamber accident Ödesvaka could not send a broad signal without my command, and I could not reach a command surface. Havets Sista Sång, the old ship, was still under Lars's old command envelope. Fastulv, the new freight ship you arrived in, could not have carried a message out either, not from inside the bubble."

"That is bad redundancy," Lars said.

Valdrun's mouth tightened. "Yes."

Lars stood too quickly, regretted it, and pretended not to. The medical display complained. He walked a small circle because sitting still had started to itch.

"Black Hole B-7 attacked me on The Rim. Vigor gave me whisky and beef. One word from Grim's old fragments nearly splits my skull. I leave for the Void. We find Station Zero. I unlock an invisible ship. We pull you out from under a two-ton distributor. I learn I am six hundred and forty-one, not Swedish in the way I thought, and that something in my blood got confused when the chamber tried to kill me."

"Mostly correct," Grim said. "You are still Swedish in several historically meaningful ways."

Lars hated that more than the rest.

"Why now? Why did it spike now?"

Valdrun gestured to the wall. The station replaced the map with a slow-moving model of the Milky Way: traffic corridors, conflict zones, collapsed colonies, energy consumption clusters, signal noise, corporate sovereignties, war fronts, quarantine regions, slave routes hidden under logistics tags, and several expanding territories marked with Black Hole B-7 influence.

"Lyra expected thresholds, not dates. When enough indicators crossed, hidden systems would begin pushing dispersed people toward return paths: violence, artifact trafficking, searches for carriers, resource loops breaking, factions hunting for what they cannot understand."

Black Hole B-7 flickered on the map. Lars felt cold.

"They wanted my blood."

"Probably."

"Can they use it?"

"Not the way they think. Most likely their hosts die, their labs contaminate themselves, and they destroy data faster than they gather it. The line adapts to a host and negotiates with biology; without the right genetic and neural environment it becomes inert, defensive, or catastrophic, which is futile and still extremely dangerous."

Lars looked at the map again.

"We need the archives. Not all of them. One thread first. Lyra left records for staged reactivation. Hard records. Holojournals. Simulations. Indexes. No faith required. Some are encoded outside ordinary human visual range."

Grim looked offended. "Again."

"You can recalibrate."

"I know."

Lars picked up the coffee again. "Then we start with the archives."
