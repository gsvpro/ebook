## Chapter 4: The Sea's Last Song

This time a voice echoed on Galactic Swedish in the old ship's internal comm system.

"Welcome back, Captain Laurðinn. System diagnostics show that all primary and secondary functions operate within nominal parameters. No critical deviations have been registered during your absence. Storage capacity is fully intact, and all redundant systems are in standby. Time simulations have confirmed a continuous operation of 315 years, 7 months and 12 days. Crew protocols are updated, and I stand at your disposal for further orders. It is an honor to reactivate your command access."

Lars had, unusually, trouble finding words for a short second.

"Thanks." Then he mumbled, "That is a damn big ship."

Grim turned his tin head.

Lars cleared his throat. "All right, 315 years, hm I am 35, so wrong by a few millennia, but how can everything have held together all that time if it is true? The name reminds me of mine anyway. What is your own name, by the way?"

"Excuse me, but now you surprise me. I am booking a health check. You activated security protocol Alpha Fourteen before you handed command over to me. I am still Havets Sista Sång."

Grim said, "He has not visited the doctor in several years."

Lars looked at Grim but only said it was starting to feel cramped, could they not continue investigating the base, it felt as if they had more to discover. Grim looked at him and said, "Havets Sista Sång, see you a little later." They rounded the old ship and continued into the gloom. Grim was barely a year old. Havets Sista Sång had been watching for three hundred and fifteen, and it had its own mind. It did not take orders from Grim.

After half an hour they reached the end of the hangar. On the way they came across a number of smaller ships and various maintenance robots and machines. They glimpsed a wall console in the distance.

Lars bent his fingers with a cracking sound and put a finger on the smooth surface, which as before showed a series of rows of symbols in pale green glow. He understood nothing, but the fingers seemed to live their own life and quickly pressed the right sequence. Silently and quickly a door opening formed to the left of the console. They stepped through. As before it was like an airlock, and an opening took form when the first had closed, but at the other end of the room. They stepped out of the lock and into a long wide corridor with light bands that lit and went out over a stretch of a hundred meters as they moved. The walls were gray-white and smooth with slightly rounded corners.

"I think we are in a passage that is in the equatorial plane and goes around the station," Lars said, and pointed. "Look, there is a window."

An oblong rectangular window with rounded corners sat on the wall to their right. Outside the Milky Way glittered. Because of the way the station sat in folded space the view was a little wrong, stars shifting not with ordinary parallax, but Lars did not have a name for that. On the left they could see corridor crossings or wall consoles for doors or lifts. They reached a new crossing.

"Here we take left."

They continued now in a direction in toward the center of the station. A large spherical globe came floating toward them far away in the distance.

Lars took out and unlocked the Ragnarrök 5000. "What do we do now?"

Grim answered, "Probably a peaceful maintenance drone."

The sphere began to slow, a series of symbols blinked on the front, and it talked to them in Galactic Swedish.

"Captain Laurðinn. About time. We need help, before it is too late. Follow. It is urgent."

The drone sphere turned around, or rather began traveling back from where it came. Lars and Grim looked at each other. Lars secured the weapon and slung it on his back and adjusted the new sunglasses. They began running after the drone, and Lars called, "What is your name and what is your task?"

"My name is Ödesvaka, my task is to watch and see that the Alpha Fourteen protocol is followed, but I am unimportant, hurry now."

The protocol name jabbed in the neck, but the drone was already veering through a wide portal to the right. Ödesvaka swung left into a large hall. The lighting was weak and blinked at regular intervals. It smelled of burned bakelite and ozone. Large machines stood lined up in the hall. They began walking further in. The sphere had now stopped. It said, with a serious tone, "I stop here."

Grim's left hand twitched, then twitched again.

"I am getting noise in the control," he said.

"Stay at the door."

Grim stepped back into the corridor. Ödesvaka hovered beside him.

Lars stepped around the sphere. His right foot hit something on the floor with a metallic clang. He looked down and saw the hand of a robot. He peeked further into the haze and started. In a line out from the machine furthest left lay at least ten robots and five drones, twisted and broken. The machine looked largely intact except a large hatch that looked as if it had come loose. Most of the broken robots lay in a pile at the machine furthest right, and that machine had a large heavy power distributor that had collapsed. Under it lay an android-like figure, pinned.

The android had now spotted them.

"Thanks for coming. I must warn the robot, do not go further, it is not healthy here for robots." A tired laugh was heard, then the android gathered himself with a rattling breath. "I have been stuck here a long time. The nanobots make me survive. They can convert the energy to everything I need. I think I now know the cause and the sequence of the accident. I have had time to analyze."

"Lars is my name."

"I already know that. My name is Valdrun. We need cables and pulleys so we can create a lever effect and lift the power distributor. There is a workshop to the right of the exit. There you should be able to find or build what is needed. The power distributor is heavy, it weighs two tons."

He had a human face, mostly. Tall, blue-pale, with skin that looked too cool and too strong. One side of the skull and neck showed integrated material, not a prosthesis in any ordinary sense. Long arms, broad shoulders, a face like a Viking statue. He spoke slowly, the way a man does when he has been doing this work for centuries.

Lars and Grim ran out to the workshop. After some searching they found what they needed. They calculated the problem.

"We need a counterweight." Lars called to Ödesvaka. "Is there any large work robot? What did you use to place the equipment here?"

"It was built in place, but we have work robots that can lift several tons. But we dare not sacrifice more robots."

"If the cables reach, the robot can stand at the door. Is there any durable place in the ceiling we can put the pulley in?"

"There is a beam inside the ceiling surface and a service space inside the walls. You can reach it through a hidden hatch in the workshop."

Lars lifted his hands. "Where? Ah, all right, now it opened. Bring the pulley and the tools."

The hatch had vanished when his left hand pressed a blank surface, a little before he had decided to press it. He took off through the service shaft and up into the ceiling gap. Heat increased as he crawled. The air tasted metallic. When he thought he was right over the machine he shouted to the sphere, "How do I open the ceiling?"

"Put your hand against the surface."

Lars pressed his hand against the surface. A small square of one square meter suddenly disappeared and he looked down on the machine below, wreckage, Grim at the safe threshold, Valdrun pinned under the distributor looking up.

First he fastened the pulley's one end with a cable around the beam and tightened the bolt, then he pulled a long line he had brought up through the pulley, tied it, and dropped it down through the hole in the ceiling.

Lars began to feel a bit dizzy from the fumes from the facility below, but he was done and hurried unsteadily back through the cramped space. He puffed; never had he felt this weak. With a hoarse voice he gave new commands.

"Grim, hold the end of the cable and give it to the work robot. I will pull the cable through now."

The heavy work robot arrived in the corridor like a walking loading crane. Lars took one end of the cable and stumbled forward through the pile of robots and sheet-metal parts and tied the line to the cable and began quickly pulling on the other end of the line; at last the cable was through the pulley and he began threading it around the fallen power-distributor construction. The surface was hot through the gloves. The suit alarm flashed yellow, then orange.

"It seems worse than I thought, you look affected too but not as fast," said Valdrun with a serious voice.

"I have never felt this weak. Keep your fingers crossed now." Lars coughed, turned around, and staggered quickly back while he focused on taking one step at a time. He recalculated while he walked. If the robot pulled now, the distributor would drag sideways across Valdrun's pinned side. He needed lift first, then a little sideways movement. He attached a second line, nearly fell, wrapped it around a floor anchor that surfaced when he kicked the right panel.

"Now, start pulling but carefully, we do not know what the pulley can take even if it looked as if it had the right dimensions. Ten centimeters lift. No more."

The work robot pulled. The distributor rose with a deep sound. Valdrun inhaled sharply.

"Hold. Guide line left. Slow."

The distributor shifted away from the torso by a centimeter, then three, then six. The overhead beam screamed. Lars stumbled forward, grabbed Valdrun under one arm, and pulled. Valdrun's free hand dug into the floor. His damaged side sparked once, then locked. They came free together.

"Release the load."

The work robot eased the distributor down. It struck the floor with a deep metallic boom.

For one second everything held. Then everything went black for Lars.
