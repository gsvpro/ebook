## Chapter 2: The Farmer

They went through the connecting corridor toward the marketplace, which was like a gigantic sphere. They stepped in. Clouds sailed around in the sky, birds were singing, and high up on the other side Lars glimpsed a grazing herd of cows. He pointed.

"That's where we're going."

Lars opened the gate to the farm, sighed with relief, and walked along the gravel path. He whispered to Grim, who walked beside him with whirring servo motors.

"Now you have to behave a bit stiff and tinny."

Grim stared back with a sour look.

"Why? Do I get to eat anyway?"

"Yes you do, but we don't want to show all our cards."

Grim had originally been a ship computer built with human brain cells, fed with a nutrient solution. It had started as a discussion one day two months earlier. Grim had said he wanted to be able to eat as well, and to taste things.

Lars had answered that he did not need that, it was only an evolutionary survival strategy so we would not poison ourselves, but if he absolutely wanted it he had a free hand. Draw up a sustainable plan and they would look at it later.

The next day Grim had a thoroughly worked-out solid plan with drawings and everything. They were in orbit around Alpha Boötis to take on fuel. The red glow lit up the cabin. Lars stood examining the drawings on the screen. The ghostly red light reflected in his face.

"Good. You've thought about checking for toxic substances," he said. "And there's a shopping list."

"Most of it, probably all of it, can be 3D printed," Grim answered over the comm.

Two days later, when Lars walked through the connecting passage from the engine room to the bridge, he smelled cooking and heard smacking from inside the computer room. On the table by the keyboard stood a plate of pepper steak and potato gratin, and a metallic mouth was busily shoveling in meat with small manipulator arms.

"Fantastic. I see you need proper robot arms and eyes. But you ought to practice etiquette."

"I was so eager to test it that I asked the cook to make it. What an experience."

"But no Xaltor X whisky or wine with it. Your brain can't take that, and we have to ration the food. You can have half of the sirloin, the salmon, and the rhubarb compote, but the rest will be astronaut food. All right, think about how we can build you into a robot."

Lars's stomach had started to rumble, but he finished first by checking how the helium collection was going.

Now he looked up and saw a farmer in a hat, red-checked shirt, denim overalls, and green boots coming toward them.

"Welcome. Vigor Hallman. What can I do for you?"

They introduced themselves, and Lars continued.

"We're looking for farm sales of beef."

The farmer nodded and peered at them with a sly look.

"Then you've come to the right place. Do you want to eat here too?"

"Gladly, if that's all right. And we were thinking of a large load to stock the ship, closer to sixty kilos."

"Come this way," said the farmer, turned, and started walking. They went around the main building and over to a sales room. Straw crunched under their boots when they stepped into the timber cottage. It smelled of straw from the straw-covered floor, and of vegetables. Dried meat hung from the ceiling. In cooled display shelves lay vacuum-packed meat in long rows, and there were plenty of vegetables and root crops for sale. Wine bottles stood lined up along the wall shelves. Robots served the customers quickly but with care.

"We have everything," Vigor said. "We are completely self-sufficient."

"Oh. You've got something big going on, I see."

Vigor looked at Lars, laughed, and said, "People here appreciate real well-cooked food without semi-manufactured stuff. Three years ago I was close to panic. Demand exceeded supply. I couldn't keep up and thought about shutting the whole thing down. But I couldn't do that, so I made a plan to automate and industrialize most of it, and now I can spend more time with the animals as well, and we have fivefolded the sales."

"Wow. What marbling," Lars said.

They walked around and pointed at what they wanted, then the farmer chased them out onto the lawn outside, where there were tables and chairs under parasols, and said they should sit down while the robots packed it up. He paused.

"You can be my guests. If you have a moment, I'll buy you dinner. We don't often have guests from far away."

He glanced at Grim and asked Lars, "Does the robot eat too?"

"Yes, unfortunately. He's picked up a few habits."

Grim said, "I am not an it, and we would like pepper-fried sirloin and potato gratin if that's possible."

Vigor clapped his hands.

"Fantastic. Very good choice. We'll arrange that. In the meantime I offer you a surprise."

Two waiter robots in white clothes came out through the door carrying trays with two whisky glasses and a cordial glass, and set a whisky glass in front of Lars and a cordial glass in front of Grim. The farmer sat down opposite where the robot had set the last glass. Another robot came out and set down a jug of elderflower cordial and an unmarked bottle of whisky, glanced at Grim, and then went in.

Lars stiffened when he recognized the shape of the bottle. Upset, he hissed at Vigor.

"Is this some kind of test? I have had enough ridiculous stunts today."

The farmer peered back slyly under the hat.

"It is not a test, and you can be calm. You are among friends. I recognize a like-minded person when I see one."

Lars had begun to tremble a little, so Vigor continued.

"I can tell you that the whole farm is protected against eavesdropping and espionage, and we can defend ourselves, maybe not as well as you." He went on, glancing at Lars's Ragnarrök 5000. "The robots have several roles. They help with patrolling and various guard duties. I am not only a simple farmer myself. I am also an adviser to the station's governor."

Quickly he showed that the beard was a false beard.

Lars began to get the anger under control. He had scanned the surroundings and had discovered a few well-hidden defensive positions. He began to see the small details: the robots' precision, Vigor's precise catlike movements, which he tried to hide, just as Lars himself had learned. Strangely, Vigor began to look familiar, and he got a déjà vu, as if he had sat here before in a vanished time. He must not think about that, or a disabling headache would arrive as surely as a letter. He already felt a diffuse pain in his neck.

He leaned back, took a deep breath, let it out, rubbed his face, and said, "Sorry for my abrupt manner. It has been a long day. Let's toast."

They enjoyed the dulling effect of the Xaltor X. Grim sipped the cordial and said after a while, "What is this juice called? I have never drunk anything better."

"Elderflower cordial," said the farmer, and laughed.

"Ohh, can we take a hundred liters of elderflower cordial, Lars?"

"Of course," said Lars and Vigor in unison.

Lars had taken off the graphite-kevlar composite coat and inspected the shoulder. The work overall had melted into the coat exactly where the plasma and radiation had hit him. At last he got the coat free and could inspect the shoulder. It looked as usual. Not a scratch.

"Stay there. I'll be right back," said the farmer.

Lars lay down on the lawn and looked up at the clouds and the swallows. Grim lay down beside him.

"What a green oasis, on the edge of the KBC Void."

Lars avoided saying, or thinking, that he had never felt more at home than now.

He woke with a start. For a microsecond he wondered where he was, until he smelled the pepper-fried sirloin. He opened his eyes and looked over toward the table, where a row of robots had begun to set places. He knocked on Grim, which produced a tinny sound, and whispered, "Have you fallen asleep?" Grim had a funny habit of always imitating what he did. At the same time he stretched so it creaked, and walked toward the farmer, who had just come out through the door.

Vigor said, "Now you will taste the KBC Void's, and probably the galaxy's, best sirloin."

Lars glanced irritably at Grim, who with precise catlike movements had already passed him and sat down at the table without even a squeaking servo. Lars pulled out his chair, sat down, and leaned toward Grim. With a serious face he said, "That sort of thing can have serious consequences, not only for you. Never drop your guard."

Grim looked at him but could not get a sound out. Vigor peered at them and said, "It is easy for us, but hard for a robot who is barely dry behind the ears."

Serving robots in white waiter costumes poured wine and served pepper steak, potato gratin, and salad. They toasted and dug into the food.

Lars said, with sarcasm, "The only thing missing is live candles."

He had a blissful look when he looked up.

"This is probably the best sirloin I have eaten, if not the best dinner in a very long time," he said, with a dreaming look.

Grim turned his head toward Lars with jerky servo-squeaking movements and said, "I can probably agree with that completely."

Vigor chuckled and said, "That gladdens me to hear. I understood it when it got so quiet. Cheers, my friends."

They toasted with ringing wine glasses. Even Grim had been given a half-full wine glass.

After dinner Vigor slurped his coffee and said, "So you are stocking the finest meat and wines for the next destination?"

"And elderflower cordial," Grim added.

Lars did not know why he was so frank.

"Yes, it will probably be a small trip. I don't really know why, but we feel a bit hunted."

Vigor lifted his hat a little and scratched himself.

"Well, you have sharp claws and good allies anyway, so it should go all right. If you need anything you can count on me, and on the station too."

"Got a request to billet the Eleventh Aphex Defence Brigade overnight. They were apparently stranded."

"Ah, Elliot Fabron. Well, he is not so cocky anymore."

They began to walk down toward the main gate, followed by two pack robots with carts full of goods.

Vigor said, "In a few weeks this sector will be crawling with galactic police and Aphex Defence forces."

"Then we'll be far away."

"If you see Elliot, can you point the way here?"

"Absolutely," Lars said, and shook Vigor's hand, and gave him a hug. Grim did exactly the same.

When they had come a bit out on the road from the farm they saw a small column of people in gleaming armor coming over the hill. A tight-faced Elliot met Lars's gaze. Lars pointed.

"See the gate over there. Follow the gravel path up toward the farm. You will be well looked after."

He clapped Elliot on the shoulder.

"Take care of yourself. See you a little later."

Lars, Grim, and the pack robots with levitating carts continued down the hill. The gravel crunched, swallows hummed in the air, and a cow lowed. Clouds sailed on the clear blue sky. A bit away, water poured from a waterfall.

They did not go back to the pub. They went to the ship, loaded the beef, the wine, the elderflower cordial, and new sunglasses Grim had already bought from a kiosk between a knife stall and a noodle stall, and filed a civilian departure. Eight minutes later they left that vector.

Lars did not say much. The back of his neck still ached, not sharply, more as if something behind a locked door had leaned against it. Ahead of them the KBC Void opened, an absence large enough that the instruments treated it as an error.

"Just a short look," he said.

Grim made a sound that might have been a laugh, or a servo.

"With you, that has never once been true."
