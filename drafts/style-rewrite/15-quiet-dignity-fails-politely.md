## Chapter 15: Quiet Dignity Fails Politely

The dart accelerated at two hundred gravities. No human pilot, and no ordinary missile either. It made one correction, then split into six smaller objects, each one leaving almost no heat trail. They were kinetic penetrators with smart skins. Grim said they probably carried sensor dust. Valdrun said they might carry a blood-sample harvester if Black Hole B-7 had become especially disgusting this century. Lars did not reach for a chair because there still was no chair.

"Options," he said.

Valdrun said capture one. They needed to know how far the containment technology had progressed.

Havets Sista Sång displayed intercept curves. The six darts spread into a flower pattern, trying to force the ship either to reveal itself through acceleration or take a hit. Their guidance systems were not seeing Havets directly. They were attacking the empty place where the relay had died.

Lars asked if they could spoof the darts into hitting each other, capture one at the same time, and do it without a light show visible from The Rim. The ship said yes to all three.

The darts closed. Havets Sista Sång did not fire a gun and it did not dodge. There was nothing to see from outside, no beam, no flash. The old ship put false targets into the darts' steering, so four of them chased empty places in the dust. Two of those struck each other and became vapor. The others shredded themselves in the hauler's empty ice tanks.

One dart kept coming. Valdrun wanted that one. Grim marked it as the capture candidate.

"No," Lars said. "That one has a secondary pulse."

Grim checked and did not see it. Lars said there was a magnetic shell under the skin. It wanted to be captured. Valdrun moved instantly. Trap inside trap. Use the other one, Lars said, third from the left, no extra pulse. Havets Sista Sång accepted the correction without comment.

The last of the ghosts went to dust. The dart Lars had chosen stopped moving. A containment bubble formed around it, barely larger than Lars's hand, holding the dart in a pocket where inertia had been set aside. It hung on the command display. Captured, the ship said. Valdrun wanted it in a hard isolation box, no network contact, no station contact, no biological contact. Grim agreed.

Lars looked at the dart's model. It had a sampler. The display peeled the device open layer by layer: kinetic core, passive sensor mesh, molecular adhesion filaments, microcapillary array, cryo-sealant, a data seed designed to transmit after collection. Blood-compatible containment had become hardware.

Then Havets Sista Sång said the device was built to take blood from someone who had not agreed. Valdrun stared at the ceiling. The old ship's archive said Captain Laurðinn had often asked for an emotional recommendation during combat. Lars said they could please focus on the blood harvester.

They dissected the captured dart in a remote isolation bay using tools controlled through three sacrificial interfaces and a dumb mechanical relay Valdrun insisted on adding. The dart had no origin stamp. It did have manufacturing habits: tolerances from Perseus-arm paramilitary shops, a NUKEA-compatible microcell stolen or copied from civilian reactor safety tech, Aphex-derived guidance math two generations old, and a biological sampling layer that made Lars want to find the designer and feed him to his own procurement department.

"They are not just hunting you," Grim said.

The dart's target model appeared as patterns rather than a face. Long-lived individuals. Abnormal survival. Unusual radiation tolerance. Financial anomalies. Medical records with missing aging markers.

Lars stared. They had built a net. Valdrun nodded. A crude one, but over ninety years crude nets caught things.

"How many of the priority nine are in it?"

Grim checked against the blurred archive list. This time the station allowed several shapes to sharpen. At least four. The room tightened.

"Locations?"

One near The Rim corridor, Grim said, possibly Vigor already shielded. One inside an Aphex research dependency. One in a NUKEA arbitration habitat. One, he paused, one in territory influenced by the Syren Collective.

Valdrun's expression turned cold.

"No."

Lars looked at him. That one waits, Valdrun said. Lars asked if Black Hole had the same net. The Syren region was not where they started. It was dangerous, yes, but more than that it was designed to turn a rescue into recruitment, martyrdom, or war.

Lars looked at the map. Syren space pulsed dark red, not large but dense, religious fragmentation, quantum technology cults, anti-human rhetoric mutated through centuries, old doomsday logic wearing new mathematics. A single dramatic intervention there could become scripture by lunchtime. The person there did not matter less. A bad rescue could still kill more than leaving them, and fail besides. He hated that.

"First target," he said. "The Aphex dependency."

Grim asked if that was because Arsenault gave them leverage. Because Aphex already knew him, already owed him, already wanted to classify everything, and already had enough force nearby to create noise. They could hide a rescue inside a corporate security audit. Valdrun nodded slowly. Limited, local, plausible.

Havets Sista Sång returned to Arkology Zero through a path that did not exist before or after it used it. The old ship settled into the hangar with the captured dart sealed in isolation and the false trail already crawling toward the dead wellness moon. Fastulv was still in the secondary hangar, lights off, looking like a freight ship that had never left. Operation Quiet Dignity had not stayed quiet in the way they had hoped. It had still done the job.

As Lars stepped off the command deck, his comm buffer lit with a delayed packet routed through three dead trade relays, an Abantu agricultural invoice, and a recipe for elderflower cordial. Vigor. The message contained only three words.

COHERENCE, OLD IDIOT?

Lars smiled. Grim asked if he should reply. Lars thought of Vigor's false beard, the farm, the whisky, the hug he had not understood.

"Send: Coherence. Also your invoice is fraudulent."

Grim sent it. Five seconds later Vigor replied. FRAUD IS A STRONG WORD BETWEEN FRIENDS. BLACK HOLE LISTENERS ARE MOVING. APHEX IS NERVOUS. YOUR FRENCHMAN HAS BEEN CALLING EVERYONE. TRY NOT TO NUKE THE RIM.

Lars sighed. Valdrun said he had used nuclear weapons within station traffic control limits, and Lars said it had worked. Ödesvaka hovered from the hangar ceiling and remarked that success did not erase traffic law.
